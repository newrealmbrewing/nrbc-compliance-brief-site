Top Story

Total Wine presses Congress to regulate THC drinks through the three-tier system

NATIONAL • Global Drinks Intel • Nov 16

Total Wine & More, which operates about 300 stores across 30 states, is pressing Congress to regulate low-dose hemp THC beverages rather than let the federal ban take effect December 11. CEO Ryan Ross said nearly 300,000 customer messages have been sent to lawmakers, and argued the products should be placed within the post-Prohibition three-tier system of licensed producers, wholesalers and retailers: “America has a safe system for wine, spirits and beer… It's time to use it for low-dose hemp-derived THC-infused beverages.”

Why it matters: The country's largest alcohol retailer is asking Congress to route THC beverages through the same licensed distributor-and-retailer channel you already operate — the clearest signal yet that, if a fix passes, the category would flow through existing three-tier partners rather than around them.

Federal

Klobuchar says she is pursuing a bipartisan fix ahead of the December 11 hemp ban

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 17

Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-MN) said a federal hemp products ban would be devastating for small businesses and jobs, and that she is pursuing a bipartisan legislative fix for Minnesota's hemp industry before the December 11 effective date. She has previously introduced bipartisan hemp bills with Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY). No fix has been scheduled for a vote in either chamber.

Around the States

Nebraska advances its first cannabis license fee schedule unchanged over licensee objections

NEBRASKA • Marijuana Moment • Nov 16

The Nebraska Medical Cannabis Commission voted unanimously to advance rule amendments that set the program's first fee schedule, declining changes requested in a joint letter from all four licensed cultivators. As proposed, every registered cannabis establishment — cultivator, product manufacturer or dispensary — would pay a $5,000 application fee, an annual license and renewal fee of $10,000 to $28,500 depending on license type, and $13,000 to participate in the seed-to-sale tracking system; dispensaries would also pay $250 per new product listing plus a $100 renewal, and relocations cost $1,500. Transporter licenses would be merged into manufacturer licenses. The amendments now go to the attorney general for constitutional review, then to Gov. Jim Pillen; they take effect five days after his signature.

Why it matters: A Nebraska licensee's first-year regulatory load would run roughly $28,000 to $46,500 per license before product fees — a cost structure any supplier or retail partner entering the program should price in now.

Maryland tells dispensaries to sort hemp inventory before the federal definition changes

MARYLAND • Maryland Cannabis Administration • Nov 13

In an updated bulletin, the Maryland Cannabis Administration said that effective December 11 licensed dispensaries may sell hemp-derived products in their public waiting areas only if the products meet the new federal definitions — including the 0.4 mg combined total THC per container cap. Products that do not qualify may be sold only in the restricted service area and only if they meet MCA manufacturing, testing, packaging and labeling requirements; dispensaries planning to return non-compliant inventory to sellers should arrange it before December 11.

Why it matters: Suppliers with hemp beverages in Maryland's dispensary channel have under four weeks to document federal compliance or arrange returns — after December 11, non-compliant product can only move through the restricted channel under full MCA requirements.

Virginia panel recommends changes to the retail cannabis law before 2027 sales launch

VIRGINIA • Marijuana Moment • Nov 16

The Joint Commission overseeing Virginia's transition to a cannabis retail market, chaired by Sen. Lashrecse Aird (D), recommended changes for the General Assembly to take up in January, including clarifying that localities can set stricter minimum distances between cannabis businesses (with limits on how far they can restrict operating hours) and barring lawmakers and their immediate families from holding ownership stakes in the industry. Retail sales remain scheduled to begin July 1, 2027; the panel deferred on-site consumption and event-permit licensing to a 2027 study.

New Haven asks Connecticut to let cities ban small liquor bottles

CONNECTICUT • WFSB • Nov 16

New Haven's Board of Alders is sending the governor and state lawmakers a resolution asking them to amend the state Liquor Control Act of 1933 so individual municipalities can prohibit sales of “nips,” citing litter and emergency-call reductions reported by Massachusetts towns with similar bans. The city logged more than 2.3 million nips sold from April through September under its 5-cent surcharge; a local store manager said nips are about half his business and opposed a ban. Any change would require state legislation.

On the Radar

Dec. 11 — The amended federal hemp definition takes effect nationwide: finished products with more than 0.4 mg combined total THC per container lose hemp status, covering most THC beverages now on shelves, unless Congress passes a fix or a further delay first.

Nov. 19 — Illinois's court-ordered enforcement pause on the state hemp THC restrictions expires for the roughly 250 businesses covered by the pending lawsuit; absent an extension, dispensary-only rules apply to them as well.

DEA rescheduling — Final-phase briefs are in on moving marijuana to Schedule III; the administrator's decision has no deadline, and DEA's proposed 2027 production quotas remain open for comment.
