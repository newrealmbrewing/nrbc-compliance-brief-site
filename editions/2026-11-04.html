Top Story

Massachusetts voters reject Question 8; retail cannabis sales to remain legal

MASSACHUSETTS • Boston.com / WGBH • Nov 3

Voters rejected the ballot question that would have made recreational marijuana illegal again, repealed home-grow allowances and changed possession penalties. The Associated Press projected the no votes would prevail; with roughly 42% counted, about 76% opposed repeal. Adult-use establishments grossed more than $1.65 billion in Massachusetts in 2025, per the Cannabis Control Commission; opponents of the measure, including cannabis-beverage maker LEVIA, had said passage would have been “devastating” to their businesses, while supporters cited concerns about child-appealing products and accidental ingestion.

Why it matters: The state's licensed adult-use market continues uninterrupted — no wind-down of retail licenses, beverage SKUs or distribution accounts in Massachusetts, and existing compliance obligations stay as they are.

Federal

Hemp law's synthetic-cannabinoid provisions take effect Nov. 12; broader THC redefinition follows Dec. 11

FEDERAL • Foley Hoag LLP • standing deadline

With the midterms decided, the 2025 hemp law's deadlines are the next fixed dates on the federal calendar: cannabinoids that the hemp plant cannot naturally produce become illegal November 12 (the 30-day extension Congress passed in September did not apply to them), and hemp products containing more than 0.4 mg of total THC per container are redefined as Schedule I marijuana on December 11 unless Congress acts first.

Why it matters: Distributors and retailers holding products with lab-created cannabinoids need them off shelves by Nov. 12; hemp-THC beverages above the 0.4 mg threshold face the Dec. 11 date — plan inventory and returns now rather than in December.

Around the States

Georgia raises medical cannabis card fee from $30 to $75, effective Dec. 5

GEORGIA • Georgia Dept. of Public Health • Nov 3

Registration fee change — full schedule: DPH announced the Medical Cannabis (Low THC Oil Registry) card fee rises from $30 to $75 per card at 12:01 a.m. on December 5, 2026. The fee applies per card to new, renewal and replacement cards, and both patients and caregivers must each hold a card; cards remain valid for five years. The online portal service fee also rises from $3.75 to $4.88 per transaction (district release). No licensing-tier changes for manufacturers, dispensaries or distributors were announced.

Why it matters: In our home state, a patient-and-caregiver pair will pay $150 per five-year cycle after Dec. 5, up from $60 — a 150% increase in registry cost that retail partners serving registered patients should factor into enrollment support before the deadline.

Idaho voters reject HJR 4, keeping citizen initiatives on drug policy

IDAHO • Idaho State Journal / Idaho Capital Sun • Nov 3

Voters rejected the constitutional amendment 67.2%–32.8% (as of late election night; AP called the race at 10:39 p.m. MT). HJR 4 would have given the Legislature sole power to legalize any drug or psychoactive substance and ended ballot initiatives on the subject. Legislative supporters said it would keep out-of-state groups from financing marijuana measures; opponents argued voters would be giving up their own initiative rights. Marijuana remains illegal in Idaho; certification is due November 24.

Houston approves Prop A: misdemeanor marijuana becomes lowest police enforcement priority

TEXAS • KPRC Click2Houston • Nov 4

Houston voters approved Proposition A, directing that misdemeanor marijuana possession be the “lowest possible enforcement priority” for city police and banning no-knock warrants; early returns showed roughly 72% in favor (partial count). The measure changes city enforcement priorities only — state law on possession and licensing is unchanged.

Double Oak, Texas votes to stay dry, rejecting local alcohol sales

TEXAS • Cross Timbers Gazette • Nov 3

Voters in Double Oak (Denton County) rejected a citizen-petitioned local option measure to allow alcohol sales, 55.6% to 44.4% (1,020–814). The measure was tied to a planned commercial development expected to include a grocery store and restaurants; under Texas local-option rules the question cannot return for at least a year.

On the Radar

Nov. 12 — Illinois' reclassification of hemp-THC beverages takes effect, moving the products out of general retail; the federal ban on non-naturally-occurring (synthetic) cannabinoids takes effect the same day.

Nov. 29 — DEA's supply-chain sourcing deadline for federally registered medical cannabis dispensaries under the Schedule III framework.

Dec. 4 & Dec. 11 — Rhode Island hemp inventory reports are due Dec. 4; the federal hemp redefinition (products over 0.4 mg total THC per container become Schedule I) takes effect Dec. 11 unless Congress acts in the lame-duck session.
