Good morning — here's the regulatory picture for alcohol and hemp/THC beverages, readable in about three minutes. Every headline links to the original source. A quiet 24 hours on the federal side, so today's edition runs lighter than usual.

TOP STORY

New Mexico's tracking cutover hits its final deadline today — with the court challenge still undecided

NEW MEXICO • KUNM • Sep 1

Today is the day New Mexico retailers lose access to the state's outgoing BioTrack system, completing the forced cutover to the new state-built NMS2S seed-to-sale platform. At an Aug. 31 hearing on the industry's bid to halt the transition, Second District Judge Joshua Allison declined to rule immediately, saying he would first observe how the new system performs for retailers; the Cannabis Association of New Mexico said the shutdown period had already interrupted sales, with executive director Matt Kennicott saying affected operators went days with "no cash flow." The state's BioTrack contract expires Sept. 15.

Why it matters: From today, New Mexico retail partners must run all inventory, manifests and sales reporting in NMS2S — and with the litigation unresolved, requirements could still shift on short notice, so keep exported BioTrack records reconciled and on hand.

AROUND THE STATES

California reports $8.4 billion in cannabis tax revenue as state announces enforcement seizures

CALIFORNIA • Marijuana Moment • Sep 2

Gov. Gavin Newsom announced the state has collected $8.4 billion in cannabis taxes since legalization — nearly $4.5 billion in excise and about $3.4 billion in sales tax, including $261.7 million in Q2 2026 — and officials said August enforcement operations seized $13.3 million in illicit marijuana and tobacco products. Newsom said the state is "going after the illicit operators" who undermine the legal market; CDTFA director Trista Gonzalez said the seizures protect "law-abiding licensed businesses."

Missouri recalls licensed manufacturer's products over chemically modified and synthetic cannabinoids

MISSOURI • Missouri DHSS • Sep 2

The state's Division of Cannabis Regulation issued a public recall of products from GF Extraction Lab LLC, saying they "contain cannabinoids produced through chemical modification, conversion, or synthetic derivation of cannabinoids."

Why it matters: Converted and synthetically derived cannabinoids are the same product category at the center of the federal hemp-ban debate — state-level enforcement is already active, so ingredient-sourcing documentation from suppliers is worth confirming now.

ON THE RADAR

Dec. 11 — the new effective date of the federal hemp THC ban under the delay signed into law this week. Bipartisan regulation bills are pending, including the Beverage Regulatory Parity Act (Reps. Landsman and Van Duyne — three-tier distribution, TTB permits, 21+ sales, excise on THC content) and the Lawful Hemp Protection Act (Reps. Barr and Craig).

Oct. 15 — payment processor Square's deadline for merchants to remove CBD and hemp-derived items from their catalogs; the company told sellers such sales "will no longer be permitted" on its platform, citing the federal law.

Texas — a Travis County judge's decision remains pending on the hemp industry's request to pause enforcement of the state's new THC standards that took effect July 31; the Texas Hemp Business Council's Mark Bordas said enforcement risks "widespread, irreparable damage" to an $11 billion industry.
