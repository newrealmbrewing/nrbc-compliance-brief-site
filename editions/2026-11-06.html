Top Story

Missouri sues four retailers over alleged marijuana-as-hemp sales as federal court upholds state hemp law

MISSOURI • KY3 • Nov 4

Attorney General Catherine Hanaway filed suits in state circuit courts against four retailers — in Aurora, Des Peres, Independence and Rock Port — alleging they sold marijuana products presented as legal hemp and marketed themselves as licensed dispensaries; the suits seek civil penalties and injunctions, and the claims have not yet been ruled on by a court. The same week, the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Missouri dismissed an industry challenge to HB 2641, the state law redefining hemp, which plaintiffs had argued was preempted by the federal Farm Bill. HB 2641 takes effect November 12. Hanaway said, “Missourians deserve honesty and legality from the businesses operating in their communities.”

Why it matters: With the preemption challenge dismissed, Missouri's hemp restrictions take effect Nov. 12 on schedule — retail partners there need intoxicating hemp products above the state threshold off shelves by that date, and this week's suits show the AG is moving against retail accounts directly.

Federal

Illinois leaders ask new Congress to let the federal hemp THC ban take effect without further delay

FEDERAL / ILLINOIS • Marijuana Moment • Nov 5

Gov. JB Pritzker, Lt. Gov. (and Senator-elect) Juliana Stratton and Sen. Tammy Duckworth released a roughly 200-point post-election agenda for Congress that includes letting the federal ban on intoxicating hemp take effect, urging lawmakers to “oppose any further delay and any further amendment.” The ban — a 0.4 mg total-THC-per-container cap — is currently set for December 11 after Congress approved a 30-day extension in September; backers of that extension said they wanted time to write hemp regulations instead, and hemp industry groups have pressed Congress for a regulated framework rather than the ban.

Why it matters: Whether the Dec. 11 date holds, moves again, or is replaced with a regulatory framework determines how long hemp-derived THC beverages can stay in national distribution — and the new Congress now decides.

Around the States

Minnesota opens 2027 hemp license applications with a revised fee schedule

MINNESOTA • KTTC / Minnesota Department of Agriculture • Nov 5

The Minnesota Department of Agriculture opened online applications for 2027 hemp grower and processor licenses, due April 30, 2027. License fee and structure details: the base license (covering growing and processing) is a $400 minimum per company; each additional growing or processing location is $250; the separate $250 processor license fee has been eliminated; a 5% license surcharge applies to fund the agency's technology systems; and department-collected official samples run $100 per sample, with pre-harvest THC testing required for each lot. The license covers cultivation and processing only — selling or manufacturing hemp-derived cannabinoid products requires separate authorization. Anyone growing hemp indoors after Dec. 31, 2026 must renew before year-end.

Why it matters: For suppliers feeding hemp-beverage production, dropping the separate processor fee saves $250 per company, while multi-site operations should budget $250 per added location plus the 5% surcharge — a two-site grower-processor pays about $682 for 2027 versus $945 under the old structure.

Mississippi's Pearl River County votes to allow liquor, beer and wine sales

MISSISSIPPI • WLOX • Nov 4

Voters passed both countywide referendums — one on alcoholic liquors and one on beer and light wine — each with roughly 64.5% approval in preliminary results, reversing a rejection from about two decades ago. Petition organizer Sidney Albritton said the ban had pushed spending and tax revenue into neighboring Louisiana communities. Implementation timing and permitting details were not yet announced.

Why it matters: A formerly dry county on the Louisiana line opens to licensed alcohol sales — new retail and on-premise accounts for distributors once state permitting begins.

On the Radar

Nov. 12 — State hemp laws take effect in Illinois and Missouri: hemp-derived THC beverages above the 0.4 mg total-THC threshold come off liquor-store, grocery and restaurant shelves (dispensary channels remain), and Missouri's HB 2641 brings similar products under its cannabis framework.

Dec. 11 — The federal hemp redefinition takes effect for most products (0.4 mg total THC per container cap) after September's 30-day congressional extension; products with cannabinoids the cannabis plant cannot naturally produce stay on the original Nov. 12 timeline.

Michigan wholesale tax — Regulators will open e-services for the state's new marijuana wholesale tax starting Nov. 12 — wholesale partners in Michigan should be ready to register and file through the new system.
