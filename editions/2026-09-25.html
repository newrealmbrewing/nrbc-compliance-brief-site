Top Story

Import ban on Canadian packaged alcohol takes effect Monday at 12:01 a.m.

U.S.–CANADA • Brewers Association • Sep 23

The U.S. prohibition on imports of most Canadian alcoholic beverages, issued September 9 under Section 338 of the Tariff Act in response to Canadian provincial restrictions on American alcohol, takes effect at 12:01 a.m. ET on Monday, September 29. The ban covers beer produced in Canada and shipped in bottles, cans, kegs and other finished packages — regardless of brand ownership or headquarters location — while bulk beer shipped for U.S. packaging is exempt from the ban but remains subject to the existing 50 percent Section 338 tariff, and non-alcoholic beer is excluded. The Brewers Association advised importers to confirm production location, packaging format and U.S. entry timing with customs brokers, noting that shipments sent before the deadline may still face restrictions if they are entered afterward.

Why it matters: Retailers and distributors carrying Canadian-produced SKUs have two business days to land in-transit inventory; anything entered after Monday morning is barred at the border, so entry timing, broker confirmations and shelf substitutions need to be settled this week.

Federal

Senate amendment would bar student-athlete deals promoting alcohol, marijuana and gambling

U.S. SENATE • Marijuana Moment • Sep 25

Sen. Tim Scott (R-SC) filed an amendment to a college sports bill that would prohibit name-image-likeness agreements with student athletes that promote “gambling, tobacco or nicotine products, marijuana, alcoholic beverages, or sexually explicit commercial products or services.”

Why it matters: If adopted, beverage-alcohol brands would be shut out of the growing college NIL sponsorship channel — a marketing avenue some beer and seltzer brands have begun testing.

Around the States

Nebraska attorney general says he won't keep challenging the medical cannabis law

NEBRASKA • Marijuana Moment • Sep 25

Attorney General Mike Hilgers said he will not continue challenging Nebraska's voter-approved medical cannabis law after losing at the state Supreme Court, which this month rejected a standing-based challenge. The ruling clears the Medical Cannabis Commission to proceed with implementation; four cultivators have been licensed and rules for manufacturers and dispensaries are in development.

Why it matters: A new regulated market moves closer to opening, with dispensary licensing — and the compliance framework for products sold there — expected to firm up in the coming months.

Nebraska AG subpoenas Colorado dispensary near the state line; company resists in court

NEBRASKA / COLORADO • MJBizDaily • Sep 24

Attorney General Hilgers issued a September 1 civil investigative demand to Peak North Holdings, operator of a dispensary in Sedgwick, Colorado — under 30 minutes from the Nebraska border — seeking employee details, advertising materials and research on cannabis effects. The company has petitioned a court to resist, with its attorney saying Hilgers “did not identify any law that Peak is allegedly violating.”

Why it matters: Cross-border enforcement is expanding beyond product seizures to marketing and operations of out-of-state sellers — a risk factor for any retailer or brand whose advertising reaches prohibition states.

Florida proposes new background-screening rules for cannabis business owners and employees

FLORIDA • Marijuana Moment • Sep 25

Florida health regulators filed proposed changes to the rules governing background checks for medical cannabis business employees and owners, published this week in the Florida Administrative Register. Licensees should review the notice for new fingerprinting and screening obligations.

Michigan Senate passes bill requiring school notices on cannabis, hemp and nicotine products

MICHIGAN • Marijuana Moment • Sep 25

The Michigan Senate amended and approved a House-passed bill (SB 433 docket) directing officials to develop and distribute a notice to schools on the effects of cannabis, hemp and nicotine products. The amended bill returns to the House.

On the Radar

December 11 — The federal redefinition of hemp takes effect, removing most intoxicating hemp-derived THC products, including beverages, from legal sale nationwide unless Congress acts again. States are moving on their own timelines: Missouri's state restrictions take effect November 12.

Late October — Virginia's Cannabis Control Authority is expected to open preliminary feedback on its draft adult-use retail rules, with final rules due in December, license applications opening February 1, 2027, and retail sales launching July 1, 2027.

Rescheduling — DEA's review of marijuana's Schedule III move remains pending; a GAO report flagged procedural gaps in the federal scheduling process this week, and businesses seeking interim federal protections face agency-imposed conditions.
