Top Story

North Carolina’s $3 billion hemp industry prepares for the federal THC limits

NORTH CAROLINA / FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment / NC Newsline • Oct 12

The federal hemp rules Congress enacted in 2025 arrive in two steps: on November 12, products containing cannabinoids the cannabis plant cannot naturally produce fall outside the federal hemp definition, and on December 11 only hemp products with no more than 0.4 mg of total THC per container remain lawful. North Carolina’s hemp industry — roughly $3 billion and about 16,000 jobs — is preparing for the change; a Raleigh Dispensaries review found 79 of 82 tracked shops across seven counties carry at least one affected product category. The state Senate passed a bill matching the federal limit as a backstop, House Speaker Destin Hall (R) said the House may revisit the issue after the November elections, and Gov. Josh Stein’s (D) advisory council on cannabis is due to deliver recommendations by year-end.

Why it matters: Retail and distribution partners holding hemp-THC inventory face the same sell-through decision in every state ahead of December 11 — and North Carolina shows how state backstop bills could keep the limits in place locally even if Congress moves the federal date.

Spirits council asks administration to drop remaining 10% tariff on EU cognac and liqueurs

FEDERAL • The Washington Times • Oct 12

Distilled Spirits Council president and CEO Chris Swonger said the group has pressed the White House, the Commerce Department and the U.S. Trade Representative to remove the remaining 10% tariff on European cognac and liqueurs, saying “We’re not widgets. We’re distinctive products.” The administration removed the 10% tariff on Scotch whisky within the past year and lifted the tariff on Irish whiskey about two weeks ago; the article reports no administration response on cognac and liqueurs.

Why it matters: Tariff relief on the remaining categories would ease landed costs on imported-spirits SKUs for retail partners heading into holiday purchasing.

Around the States

Washington opens public comments through Dec. 4 on medical-market certification rule

WASHINGTON • Marijuana Moment • Oct 12

Following the Liquor and Cannabis Board’s vote to accept the rulemaking petition (covered Friday), the agency is now taking public comment through December 4 on the proposed certification that would document licensees’ participation in the state’s medical market for use in federal registration, banking and insurance; the board said no proposed rule language has been drafted at this stage and that it may coordinate with the state Department of Health and federal agencies on required documentation.

Puerto Rico signs employment-protection exceptions for medical cannabis patients in transportation jobs

PUERTO RICO • San Juan Daily Star • Oct 12

Gov. Jenniffer González Colón signed Law 204-2026, which creates exceptions to the employment protections for authorized medical cannabis patients when they perform “sensitive transportation functions” — including truck drivers, school-bus operators, taxi drivers and digital-platform drivers — subject to established evidentiary requirements.

Why it matters: Employers of commercial drivers in Puerto Rico gain explicit authority for safety-sensitive employment decisions involving registered patients — relevant to any distribution fleet operating on the island.

On the Radar

Nov. 12 / Dec. 11 — Federal hemp timeline: chemically synthesized cannabinoids fall outside the federal hemp definition November 12, and the 0.4 mg total-THC-per-container limit takes effect December 11 unless Congress acts (Foley Hoag).

Nov. 3 — Election Day: Massachusetts votes on Question 8, which would end licensed adult-use retail cannabis sales statewide (Marijuana Moment).

Nov. 13 — New Jersey moves intoxicating hemp-beverage sales exclusively into the cannabis-license system; until then, ABC plenary wholesale and retail distribution licensees and Class 5 cannabis retailers may sell compliant beverages (NJ CRC).
