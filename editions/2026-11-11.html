TOP STORY

Illinois hemp THC ban takes effect Thursday for businesses outside the lawsuit pause

ILLINOIS • Shaw Local • Nov 10

Illinois's new hemp law, Senate Bill 3222, takes effect Thursday, November 12. It bans delta-8 THC products and places intoxicating hemp — including THC beverages above 0.4 milligrams of THC — under the state's cannabis laws, restricting their sale to licensed dispensaries. In a joint court filing, the Attorney General's office and other state defendants agreed not to enforce the law until November 19 against the roughly 250 businesses in the Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association's federal lawsuit; a hearing on the group's emergency restraining order and preliminary injunction is set for that same day.

Why it matters: Illinois retailers and distributors not covered by the lawsuit pause must stop selling hemp THC beverages above the 0.4 mg threshold by Thursday; the November 19 hearing will determine whether relief extends beyond the plaintiff group.

FEDERAL

Beer Institute puts aluminum, taxes and hemp at center of agenda for new Congress

FEDERAL • Vinetur • Nov 10

The Beer Institute said it will focus its lobbying with the incoming Democratic-led Congress on aluminum costs, federal excise and beer-related tax policy, and the regulatory treatment of hemp-derived drinks, according to comments from president Brian Crawford reported by Beer Business Daily.

Why it matters: aluminum and excise tax outcomes flow directly into can costs and shelf pricing across the beer supply chain, and federal classification of hemp beverages will shape which channels can carry them.

AROUND THE STATES

Texas Democrats prefile marijuana legalization bills for 2027 session

TEXAS • Marijuana Moment • Nov 10

Ahead of the session that convenes in January, Rep. Joe Moody (D) filed HB 369 and Rep. John Bucy III (D) filed HB 362 — companion "Texas Regulation of Cannabis Act" bills creating licensed cultivation, processing, testing and retail, with a 15% tax on cultivator-to-dispensary transfers plus 20% at point of sale — while Sen. Carol Alvarado's (D) SB 237 would fund its program through business licensing and application fees instead of new excise taxes, with fee amounts left to rulemaking. Lt. Gov.-elect Vikki Goodwin (D) said she opposes a THC ban and argued the state health department's restrictive hemp rules should be undone.

Why it matters: each bill would create new license types for manufacturers, distributors and retailers; fee schedules are not yet set in the bill text, so Texas partners should watch for the licensing and fee framework as hearings begin.

North Carolina's incoming House speaker says marijuana decriminalization is a top topic

NORTH CAROLINA • Marijuana Moment • Nov 10

Incoming House Speaker Robert Reives (D) said "decriminalization of marijuana has been on top of everybody's minds" after Democrats won the state House; Senate control rests on one uncalled seat. A state commission reviewing cannabis policy — which has noted that intoxicating hemp products are widely sold but largely unregulated in the state — is due to deliver its final report by December 31.

ON THE RADAR

Dec. 11 — the federal hemp definition change made by the FY2026 agriculture appropriations law takes effect, pushed back from Nov. 12; products above the new THC thresholds lose federal hemp status (Shaw Local).

Nov. 19 — U.S. District Court hearing in Chicago on the Illinois hemp industry's restraining-order and preliminary-injunction motions; the state's enforcement pause for the ~250 lawsuit businesses expires the same day (Block Club Chicago).

DEA rescheduling — DEA says a separate Federal Register notice will set 2027 production quotas for rescheduled Schedule III marijuana products, with the broader rescheduling decision still pending after the hearing record closed (Marijuana Moment).
