Top Story

Southern Glazer’s to pay $12.5 million to resolve federal bribery investigation

FEDERAL / CALIFORNIA • U.S. Department of Justice • Sep 10

Southern Glazer’s Wine and Spirits agreed to pay $12.5 million to resolve a U.S. Attorney’s investigation (Northern District of California) into years of improper payments — cash, gift cards, flights, golf trips and luxury goods — made to alcohol buyers at major California grocery chains and concealed through false invoices generated by third-party vendors. The nation’s largest wine and spirits distributor agreed to strengthen its compliance program and to cooperate with prosecutions of current or former employees; the office noted that several California-based executives, including vice presidents, were directly involved. U.S. Attorney Craig Missakian said the office is “committed to making sure everyone plays by the same rules.”

Why it matters: Federal prosecutors — not just TTB and state ABCs — are policing pay-to-play in alcohol retail. Suppliers, distributors and retail partners should make sure buyer-facing incentives, samples and promotional spending are documented and within federal and state trade-practice rules.

Trump says he will remove the 10% tariff on Irish whiskey

FEDERAL • AP via ABC News • Sep 13

Speaking Sunday at a golf tournament in Doonbeg, Ireland, President Trump said he will take the 10% tariff off Irish whiskey — a rate he had reduced from 15% in July as part of the general EU tariff — though no implementation timing was announced. Tariffs on U.K. whiskey, including Scotch, were lifted in May. Why it matters: import pricing on Irish whiskey could ease, but the change is not effective until formal action issues — watch for the executive order and effective date before adjusting purchasing plans.

Sixth Circuit reverses $31.8M verdict, holds marijuana contracts unenforceable in federal court

FEDERAL COURTS • National Law Review (Bradley) • Sep 11

In Hello Farms Licensing MI, LLC v. GR Vending MI, LLC (No. 25-1759, decided Sep 10), the Sixth Circuit reversed a $31.8 million jury award, holding that federal courts will not enforce a contract whose performance requires committing federal crimes under the Controlled Substances Act. The panel wrote that appropriations riders and this year’s move of marijuana to Schedule III limit enforcement or add registration requirements but do not make the conduct legal. Why it matters: contracts touching state-legal marijuana (unlike federally compliant hemp) may be enforceable only in state court — forum and choice-of-law clauses deserve a fresh look from anyone with cannabis-adjacent counterparties.

Around the States

New York: governor’s office may move against THC drinks in grocery and retail stores

NEW YORK • Marijuana Moment • Sep 14

Marijuana Moment’s Monday roundup reports that the New York governor’s office may crack down on intoxicating THC drinks sold in grocery stores and other retail outlets outside the state’s licensed cannabis system. Why it matters: New York retail partners carrying hemp-derived THC beverages should confirm product and channel licensing status before restocking.

On the Radar

Dec. 11, 2026 — effective date of the federal hemp THC ban, after the 29-day delay signed September 3 as part of the federal funding measure. Unless Congress acts again, hemp products above 0.4 mg total THC per container are recriminalized on that date; the delay did not extend to synthetic cannabinoids that cannot be naturally produced by the cannabis plant.

Sept. 29 — for brewers and other excise taxpayers who pay by electronic funds transfer, September splits into three return periods under TTB’s September rules: the September 16–26 period’s return and payment are due by September 29.

Schedule III rollout — DEA registration terms for state-licensed marijuana businesses are still taking shape; recent reporting describes conditions negotiated case by case, and the Sixth Circuit’s Hello Farms ruling underscores that rescheduling by itself does not make marijuana commerce federally lawful.
