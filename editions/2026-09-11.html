Top Story

Southern Glazer's to pay $12.5 million to resolve federal bribery and false-invoicing investigation

FEDERAL / CALIFORNIA • U.S. Department of Justice • Sep 10

The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of California announced that Southern Glazer's Wine and Spirits, the nation's largest alcohol distributor, agreed to pay $12.5 million to resolve a federal investigation into years of improper payments — cash, gift cards, flights, golf trips and resort stays — made to alcohol-retailer employees to promote and maintain placement of certain products, concealed through false invoices and third-party vendors. The company admitted responsibility for the conduct. The case was investigated by IRS Criminal Investigation and TTB; TTB Assistant Administrator Anthony P. Gledhill said the case "serves as an important reminder that industry members are accountable for actions taken on their behalf by third parties."

Why it matters: Federal trade-practice enforcement around retail placement is active — suppliers, distributors and retail partners should confirm that promotional spending, incentives and third-party merchandising arrangements are documented and compliant with TTB trade-practice rules.

Federal

Census Bureau: states collected $15.8 billion in marijuana tax revenue over five years

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Sep 10

An updated U.S. Census Bureau report says states collected $3.55 billion in combined cannabis tax revenue between July 2025 and June 2026, and more than $15.8 billion since late 2021; California ($161 million) and Washington ($153.1 million) led collections in the second quarter of 2026, which totaled $987.5 million across 30 states and D.C.

Why it matters: The figures benchmark the scale of the state tax base tied to cannabis and adjacent THC-beverage categories — context for how states weigh tax treatment of hemp-derived drinks entering those markets.

Around the States

Connecticut: federal judge dismisses hemp farmers' challenge to state THC restrictions

CONNECTICUT • Marijuana Moment • Sep 11

A U.S. District Court judge in Connecticut dismissed a lawsuit by hemp growers and businesses challenging the state's restrictions on high-THC hemp products, ruling September 8 that the plaintiffs failed to establish standing; the plaintiffs had argued the state rules conflict with the 2018 Farm Bill's federal hemp definition.

Why it matters: Connecticut's retail limits on intoxicating hemp products remain in force, and Farm Bill preemption arguments continue to fail on procedural grounds — sellers should plan around state rules as written.

Illinois licenses 37 dispensaries for medical sales under law that also tightens hemp THC limits

ILLINOIS • Marijuana Moment • Sep 10

The Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation approved 37 existing recreational dispensaries to also serve medical cannabis patients — the state's biggest medical-market expansion since 2016 — under SB 3222, signed by Gov. JB Pritzker (D) in June. Marijuana Moment reports the same law also recriminalizes hemp-derived THC products above 0.4 milligrams of THC per container.

Why it matters: Hemp-THC beverage SKUs sold into Illinois face a strict per-container THC cap — producers and distributors shipping to the state should re-verify product eligibility.

On the Radar

December 11, 2026 — Effective date for most new federal hemp restrictions, after the September 2 law delayed the original November deadline; hemp-derived THC beverage makers have about three months of runway to adjust portfolios and inventory.

Marijuana rescheduling — Implementation of the Schedule III order continues after the D.C. Circuit's September 9 ruling declining to block it; watch for DEA and FDA implementation steps in the coming weeks.

November — Massachusetts voters decide a statewide measure on rolling back adult-use marijuana sales, and U.S. House action on hemp regulation is expected the same month.
