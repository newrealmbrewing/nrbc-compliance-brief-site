Good morning — here's the regulatory picture for alcohol and hemp/THC beverages, readable in about three minutes. Every headline links to the original source.

Top Story

Report: Alcohol company lobbyists urged state attorneys general to back federal hemp THC limits

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Oct 30

Politico reported that lobbyists for Anheuser-Busch, Molson Coors and Diageo encouraged state attorneys general to sign an August letter — ultimately joined by 35 state and territory AGs — asking Congress to reject any effort to delay, repeal or weaken the federal hemp THC limits scheduled to take effect December 11. On the other side, Rep. Morgan Griffith (R-VA) said he wants his hemp regulatory bill added to a reconciliation package before that date, the National Restaurant Association asked Congress to delay the beverage provisions, and President Trump has called on Congress to protect access to full-spectrum CBD products.

Why it matters: With industry groups split and no alternative bill advancing, the Dec. 11 federal limit of 0.4 mg total THC per container remains on track — retailers and distributors carrying hemp THC beverages should plan inventory, reorders and sell-through around that date.

Federal

Cannabis businesses await IRS guidance on deductions after rescheduling

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment / Missouri Independent • Oct 31

April's order moving state-licensed medical marijuana to Schedule III was expected to lift Section 280E's bar on ordinary business deductions for medical sales, but Treasury and the IRS have yet to issue guidance and listed it among more than 100 priorities for the fiscal year running through September 2027. Businesses holding combined medical and recreational licenses say they have no rule for allocating shared costs between the two channels.

Why it matters: Advisers quoted in the story suggest affected companies document cost-allocation methods now and set estimated payments they can support — the last 2026 estimated payment for C corporations is due December 15.

DEA posts daily-updated list of nearly 7,000 rescheduling applicants

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Oct 30

The DEA published a webpage listing 6,983 state-licensed medical marijuana businesses that applied on time for federal registration — 4,197 dispensaries, 710 distributors and 2,076 manufacturers across at least 43 states, D.C. and Puerto Rico. Timely applicants qualify for expedited review and may operate under their state licenses while applications are pending; the agency says the list will be updated daily. Manufacturers, distributors and labs now file on a new Form 225.

Around the States

Massachusetts votes Tuesday on Question 8, which would end recreational marijuana sales

MASSACHUSETTS • Marijuana Moment / CommonWealth Beacon • Oct 29

Spending on Question 8, a ballot measure that would eliminate the state's recreational marijuana industry, has reached nearly $6.5 million — more than $3.7 million from supporters of the repeal, led by SAM Action Inc., and more than $2.8 million from opponents.

Why it matters: A yes vote would close one of the country's larger adult-use retail markets — partners with Massachusetts exposure should watch Tuesday's result for shelf-mix and traffic effects.

Alabama medical cannabis dispensaries to reach five statewide this week

ALABAMA • Marijuana Moment / Alabama Reflector • Oct 31

State officials said dispensaries in Bessemer and Birmingham are set to open this week, bringing Alabama's total to five. The commission reported roughly $120,000–$140,000 in monthly sales across the first three locations; further licenses, including five integrated-facility licenses that would expand the count to 37, remain tied up in litigation before the Court of Civil Appeals.

On the Radar

Nov. 12 — Federal recriminalization of synthetic cannabinoids not naturally produced by the cannabis plant takes effect; the same day, Illinois's hemp overhaul moves THC beverages out of liquor stores and other unlicensed retail.

Nov. 29 — DEA deadline for federally registered dispensaries to source products only from federally registered suppliers, per agency guidance issued last week.

Dec. 11 — The federal limit of 0.4 mg total THC per container takes effect for most consumable hemp products. In Georgia, reporting puts the exposure at about 4,100 licensed consumable-hemp businesses and $600 million to $1 billion in annual retail sales.
