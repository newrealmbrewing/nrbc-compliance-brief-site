Top Story

DEA asks D.C. Circuit to reject industry challenges to its rescheduling rules

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 23

DEA filed a reply brief Monday in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit asking the court to deny consolidated petitions from medical cannabis companies challenging its October registration guidance, calling the claims "without merit." The agency said roughly 7,100 applications arrived in the initial 60-day window, that timely filers may keep operating while applications are pending, and that petitioners are effectively seeking an emergency order suspending the Controlled Substances Act's registration requirements. Petitioners' lawyers said that without relief "within a matter of days" the results "will be devastating" for their businesses, employees and patients.

Why it matters: The filing lands ahead of DEA's Nov. 29 deadline, after which registered operators must source from and sell to only registered or timely-filed businesses — a supply-chain restructuring that distribution and retail partners in medical cannabis states should track this week.

Federal

Sen. Lankford urges colleagues to let the Dec. 11 hemp THC ban take effect

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 23

Sen. James Lankford (R-OK) wrote fellow senators that "it is critical that this ban goes into effect on December 11th," arguing that a further extension would amount to nationwide marijuana legalization. On the other side, Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-MT) and Amy Klobuchar (D-MN) are backing the Hemp Guardrails Act, which would replace the ban with a 1% total-THC limit, a 21+ age floor and FDA rules within 180 days, while several House members are circulating proposals to delay the ban or tax hemp beverages like alcohol.

DoorDash brings hemp merchants to Capitol Hill ahead of the ban

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 23

DoorDash's policy arm flew hemp business operators to Washington last week for a "Hemp Lobby Day," meeting with more than 20 Senate and House offices and calling for "clear, workable federal standards," the outlet reported. The company expanded last year into delivering hemp-derived THC and CBD products, including beverages.

Why it matters: Major delivery and retail platforms lobbying for a regulated hemp beverage market signals the category's commercial channels want continuity — relevant to partners weighing post-Dec. 11 inventory decisions.

Around the States

Pennsylvania opens bidding on 10 expired restaurant liquor licenses

PENNSYLVANIA • WPXI • Nov 23

The Pennsylvania Liquor Control Board on Monday issued an invitation for bids for 10 expired restaurant liquor licenses — a retailer-tier license held by the operating licensee — from Armstrong, Carbon, Fayette, Forest, Indiana, Luzerne, Pike, Schuylkill, Somerset and Westmoreland counties. Minimum bid is $25,000 per license, with a bid surety of $5,000 or 5% of the bid, whichever is higher; winners pay the full bid within 30 days of award. Under Act 56 of 2025, a winner may transfer a license into another county for an additional fee of $50,000 (first- through fourth-class counties) or $25,000 (fifth- through eighth-class counties), capped at two auction transfers per county per year. Questions are due Dec. 14 and sealed bids by noon Jan. 25.

Why it matters: For retail partners seeking on-premise access in Pennsylvania's quota-capped counties, this is a defined entry point — budget $25,000+ per license plus up to $50,000 per location in cross-county transfer fees before build-out.

Tennessee THC drinks face the Dec. 11 federal limit despite state rules

TENNESSEE • The Tennessean • Nov 24

Most THC beverages sold legally in Tennessee under the state framework that took effect July 1 exceed the incoming federal limit of 0.4 mg total THC per container, The Tennessean reported, leaving stores, manufacturers and distributors uncertain about post-Dec. 11 inventory. One Nashville retailer said hemp drinks are about 40% of its sales; Total Wine's Patrice Webb said "regulation gives us clarity," as the chain presses Congress for a three-tier system like alcohol's.

Illinois has issued 148 medical cannabis dispensary licenses since Sept. 10

ILLINOIS • IDFPR • Nov 23

The Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation said it has issued 148 medical cannabis dispensary licenses under SB 3222's new opt-in provision, bringing dispensaries able to serve registered patients to 220 as of Nov. 19. The license is held by the retail dispensary: adult-use dispensaries in good standing can apply to add medical service, letting patients buy at the lower medical tax rate; the release states no application fee.

Why it matters: A fast-moving new license type that shifts where medical-rate cannabis is sold in Illinois — useful context for partners watching how beverage-adjacent cannabis retail footprints expand.

On the Radar

Nov. 29 — DEA's registration deadline: registered medical cannabis operators must begin sourcing only from, and selling only to, businesses that hold or have timely applied for DEA registration; emergency challenges are pending in the D.C. Circuit (see Top Story).

Dec. 11 — The federal hemp ban takes effect: products above 0.4 mg total THC per container lose federal hemp status. Bills to delay, replace or regulate remain pending, and Rep. James Comer (R-KY) has said the likeliest vehicle for a change is the next continuing resolution.

Jan. 25 — Sealed bids due at noon in Pennsylvania's restaurant liquor license auction; the PLCB posts answers to bidder questions on Dec. 21.
