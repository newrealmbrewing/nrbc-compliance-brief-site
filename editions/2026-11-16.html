Top Story

Retailers pause hemp THC drink orders as confusion spreads ahead of Dec. 11 federal change

MINNESOTA / NATIONAL • CBS Minnesota • Nov 15

With the federal hemp definition change set for December 11, Minnesota brands and retailers report broad consumer confusion over whether state-legal THC beverages are going away. Jason Dayton, co-founder of Minneapolis beverage brand Trail Magic, said retailers in many of the 24 states carrying the brand have paused orders amid the uncertainty, and a Minneapolis café stopped selling THC products after its payment processor said it would no longer handle those transactions. Minnesota Gov.-elect Amy Klobuchar called for bipartisan legislation before the deadline, saying hemp producers, retailers and consumers need certainty.

Why it matters: Order pauses and payment-processing pullbacks are reaching hemp-beverage shelves weeks before the federal effective date — partners carrying these SKUs should confirm processor policies and their own state's status now rather than on December 11.

Federal

TTB opens 60-day comment period on alcohol industry paperwork requirements

FEDERAL • Federal Register (TTB) • Nov 13

TTB published Proposed Information Collections; Comment Request (No. 100), inviting public comment under the Paperwork Reduction Act on continuing and proposed recordkeeping and reporting requirements it administers. Comments are due January 12, 2027.

Why it matters: a routine but concrete window for breweries, distributors and retailers to flag burdensome TTB filing or recordkeeping requirements directly to the regulator.

Around the States

Illinois details hemp licensing, fees and penalties under the new Hemp Act

ILLINOIS • Illinois Dept. of Agriculture, via Marijuana Moment • Nov 16

License fees & structure: an Illinois Department of Agriculture bulletin lays out the licensing scheme now in force under the Illinois Hemp Act (effective Nov. 12). Manufacturers of hemp cannabinoid products need a Hemp Product Manufacturer license at $5,000 per business for a two-year term; makers of non-cannabinoid industrial hemp goods need a $200 two-year Industrial Hemp Processor license, replacing registrations under the repealed Industrial Hemp Act, which expired Nov. 12. Retailers and distributors of compliant products (0.4 mg or less total THC per container) need no Hemp Act license but must meet testing, labeling and child-resistant packaging rules; anything above that threshold is cannabis and may be sold only by licensed dispensaries. Non-compliant hemp products face IDOA civil penalties, and unlicensed cannabis sales carry criminal exposure up to a Class X felony with fines up to $200,000.

Why it matters: Illinois retail partners can keep stocking compliant low-dose products with no new per-store license fee — the cost falls on producing businesses at $5,000 per two-year license (about $2,500 a year) — while any above-threshold inventory must move to the dispensary channel.

Incoming Kansas governor says she wants to legalize marijuana, citing federal rescheduling

KANSAS • Marijuana Moment • Nov 13

Gov.-elect Cindy Holscher (D), whose win the Associated Press called after more than a week of counting, said she supports legalizing marijuana and argued the federal rescheduling move eases the path for state action. Holscher cited polling showing strong majority support and said the state currently gets neither revenue nor regulatory guardrails. A medical cannabis bill passed the Kansas House in 2021 but died in the Senate.

Why it matters: no bill exists yet, so this is a 2027-session watch item — but any Kansas program would open a new licensed production and distribution channel in a state that has none today.

On the Radar

Dec. 11 — the federal hemp definition change takes effect: final consumer hemp products capped at 0.4 mg total THC per container. Missouri's beverage exemption and New Jersey's extended hemp-beverage sales window both run only until that date.

Nov. 19 — Illinois's court-ordered enforcement pause covering roughly 250 businesses in the hemp industry lawsuit is set to expire; the court's next move could widen or end the reprieve from the state's dispensary-only rule.

DEA rescheduling — final briefs are in before the agency's administrative law judge; a recommended ruling on moving marijuana to Schedule III is pending, with timing not yet announced.
