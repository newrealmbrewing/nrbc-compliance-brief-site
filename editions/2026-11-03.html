Top Story

Massachusetts voters decide today whether to end retail cannabis sales

MASSACHUSETTS • Marijuana Moment • Nov 2

Question 8 on today's statewide ballot would repeal the laws allowing regulated commercial sales of recreational cannabis and home cultivation, while leaving possession legal and the medical program in place. On Monday the National Association of Black Law Enforcement Officers endorsed the "No on 8" campaign, and more than 50 current and former law-enforcement officials signed a letter saying repeal "would not eliminate demand for marijuana" and would shift sales to unregulated sellers; the measure's backers petitioned it onto the ballot to end commercial sales statewide. Gov. Maura Healey (D) and Republican challenger Mike Minogue both said they oppose the measure.

Why it matters: A yes vote would wind down the state's licensed cannabis retail channel, and tonight's result is the first statewide test of a legalization rollback — a direct signal for category and shelf planning anywhere rollback campaigns are being discussed.

Federal

AMA urges FDA to keep botanical-drug decisions science-based, proposes cannabis research schedule

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 2

Responding to FDA's September request for information on botanical drug products, the American Medical Association wrote that "social and economic concerns and disputes should not be permitted to play a part" in regulatory decisions on products including cannabis and kratom, and proposed that NIH, DEA and FDA develop a "special schedule" with funding and government-supplied cannabis for registered researchers. The AMA also called cannabis "a dangerous drug and as such is a serious public health concern."

Why it matters: A dedicated research pathway would speed the evidence base federal and state agencies lean on when they set THC product standards, potency caps and labeling rules.

Around the States

Local alcohol and cannabis questions fill today's midterm ballots

MULTI-STATE • Marijuana Moment • Nov 3

In Tennessee, Meigs County votes on two liquor questions — allowing on-premises liquor and wine sales at restaurants and bars, and permitting retail package stores (beer is already sold in the county). In Oregon, voters in roughly a quarter of counties decide local measures that would tax or ban marijuana or psilocybin sales.

Why it matters: Local-option results open or close retail territory overnight — distribution partners with Tennessee and Oregon accounts should watch tonight's county returns.

New Hampshire governor candidates clash over cannabis legalization in final debate

NEW HAMPSHIRE • Marijuana Moment • Nov 2

Gov. Kelly Ayotte (R) said she opposes adult-use legalization, arguing today's higher-potency cannabis raises addiction and mental-health risks for young people; Democratic challenger Cinde Warmington said cannabis should be legalized, regulated and taxed in-state, saying residents already buy it in neighboring states and that the revenue "should stay in New Hampshire." New Hampshire remains the only New England state without legal adult-use sales, so today's race bears directly on whether a new regulated retail market opens.

On the Radar

Nov. 12 — The federal provision excluding cannabinoids the cannabis plant cannot naturally produce from the hemp definition takes effect, classifying those products as marijuana; the same week, Illinois' Hemp Act moves hemp-derived THC products, including beverages, to licensed cannabis retailers.

Dec. 11 — The federal limit of 0.4 mg of THC per consumer container takes effect (Congress extended the original November date by one month); hemp products above the limit would no longer qualify as legal hemp.

Dec. 7 — Comments due on TTB's Proposed Information Collections No. 99, covering 16 recordkeeping and reporting items including brewers' records, beer label place-of-production coding and the CBMA import refund program.
