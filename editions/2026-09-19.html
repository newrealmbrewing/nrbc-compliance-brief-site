Top Story

Michigan revokes Muha Meds maker's license, imposes record $2 million fine

MICHIGAN • MJBizDaily • Sep 18

Michigan's Cannabis Regulatory Agency revoked the license of Michigan Investments 10, manufacturer of the widely distributed Muha Meds vaporizer line, and imposed a $2 million fine — the largest the agency has levied on a licensed cannabis business. Investigators documented untested product, gaps in seed-to-sale tracking, non-functional surveillance cameras, and 105.7 pounds of product that could not be physically located; the company must cease operations by September 24. CRA Executive Director Brian Hanna said that "when a licensee disregards those requirements to this extent, the CRA will take appropriate action."

Why it matters: Retailers and distributors carrying Muha Meds products in Michigan need a plan for affected inventory before the September 24 shutdown, and the record penalty signals that tracking and testing lapses now carry license-ending consequences for supply-chain partners.

Federal

Alabama health committee withdraws objection to federal marijuana rescheduling

FEDERAL / ALABAMA • MJBizDaily • Sep 18

Alabama's State Committee of Public Health voted unanimously Thursday to withdraw its May objection to the Justice Department's April order moving state-licensed medical cannabis to Schedule III. State Health Officer Dr. Scott Harris said the committee's original comments "weren't really about what we were trying to get at, which was what are the consequences of rescheduling." The reversal removes one of the remaining state-level objections filed against the federal reclassification.

Why it matters: Fewer pending objections means less procedural friction around the Schedule III order — relevant to partners watching how rescheduling reshapes banking, insurance and tax treatment for THC-adjacent product lines.

Around the States

Massachusetts opens major overhaul of cannabis rules, including new license types

MASSACHUSETTS • Cannabis Control Commission • Sep 16

The Cannabis Control Commission began a comprehensive rewrite of its regulations, with public working sessions that started September 17 and a vote on draft regulations planned for December, followed by public comment and a hearing in early 2027 and final approval targeted for March 2027. On licensing structure, the overhaul would create a new standalone medical dispensary license type, allow adult-use-to-medical establishment transfers, and consolidate transporter licenses; it also revisits testing, delivery opt-outs, advertising and loyalty-program rules. The Commission has not yet published a fee schedule for the new or consolidated license types — those figures should arrive with the December draft.

Why it matters: Massachusetts retailers, transporters and medical operators may see their license categories redefined — and repriced — in the December draft; partners there should track the working sessions now, while the structure is still open to comment.

Delaware renames alcohol enforcement division to add marijuana oversight

DELAWARE • State of Delaware • Sep 16

Gov. Matt Meyer signed House Bill 411, renaming the Division of Alcohol and Tobacco Enforcement the Division of Alcohol, Tobacco, and Marijuana Enforcement and reclassifying its "agents" as officers. The change formalizes inspection authority over marijuana retailers that the division has exercised since Delaware's recreational sales launched; no new license obligations or fees are created.

On the Radar

Sep 29 — The executive order barring imports of Canadian beer, wine and spirits takes effect; product entered for consumption before that date remains subject to the existing 50% duty instead (Shanken News Daily). Ten days out — retailers carrying Canadian brands should have inventory and pricing plans settled.

Sep 24 — Michigan Investments 10 must cease operations under the CRA revocation order in today's top story; Michigan partners should complete any inventory decisions before that date.

Dec 11 — The federal ban on consumable hemp products above 0.4 mg total THC per container takes effect, after Congress moved the date back from Nov 12 in this month's continuing resolution; synthetic cannabinoids not naturally producible from the plant are recriminalized on the original Nov 12 date (Marijuana Moment).
