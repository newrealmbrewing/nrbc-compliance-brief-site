Top Story

Texas hemp groups sue DSHS over THCA rule and sharply higher license fees

TEXAS • MJBizDaily • Oct 23

The Texas Hemp Constitutional Alliance and manufacturer Green Nation filed suit against the Texas Department of State Health Services, arguing the agency exceeded its authority with consumable-hemp rules, in effect since March 31, that count THCA toward the delta-9 THC limit and sharply raise license costs; the plaintiffs seek an injunction blocking enforcement. DSHS has said the fees cover program administration and enforcement, including inspections and lab testing. License fees & structure: under the adopted rules, the annual fee for a consumable hemp manufacturer/processor rose from $258 to $10,000 per facility, and the annual retail registration rose from $155 to $5,000 per location, with a $1,000 late-renewal penalty — every business manufacturing or selling consumable hemp in Texas must hold one (fee schedule).

Why it matters: The rule stays in effect while the challenge is pending — a 10-store Texas retail network now owes $50,000 a year in registrations (versus $1,550 before), and distributors' Texas manufacturing partners owe $10,000 per facility, so partners should budget for the fees rather than wait on the courts.

Federal

UPS to require THC testing documentation on hemp shipments starting Dec. 11

FEDERAL • Ganjapreneur • Oct 22

Ahead of the federal hemp-definition change, UPS updated its shipping policy: beginning December 11, hemp shipments must carry a certificate of analysis showing total THC — including THCA — of no more than 0.3% on a dry-weight basis, ship with Adult Signature Required, and move under a dedicated UPS account with a signed carrier agreement. The carrier prohibits synthetic or lab-made cannabinoids and all hemp vape/aerosolized products across its U.S. network.

Why it matters: Distribution partners shipping hemp beverages via UPS need compliant COAs, account agreements and signature service set up before Dec. 11 to avoid refused or returned freight.

Virginia Senate finance chair indicted on federal marijuana and racketeering charges

FEDERAL / VIRGINIA • Marijuana Moment • Oct 22

An eight-count federal indictment unsealed Thursday charges Sen. L. Louise Lucas (D-Portsmouth), Virginia's Senate president pro tempore and Finance chair, with racketeering, marijuana-distribution, money-laundering and honest-services-fraud conspiracies tied to retail cannabis stores; prosecutors allege the operation distributed at least 100 kilograms of marijuana. Attorney General Todd Blanche said her "first duty should have been looking out for her constituents before herself"; Lucas denied the charges and said she will not be "intimidated, bullied, or silenced."

Why it matters: Federal prosecutors remain active against unlicensed marijuana retail even while rescheduling advances — a reminder that retail partners' product sourcing must stay within licensed, compliant channels.

Around the States

New York opens first adult-use nursery license applications November 5

NEW YORK • MJBizDaily • Oct 22

The Office of Cannabis Management will take the first 50 applications, in order received, for a new wholesale-only nursery tier covering seeds, clones and immature plants. License fees & structure: a $1,000 non-refundable application fee plus an annual license fee of $750 (outdoor, up to 100,000 sq ft), $1,000 (mixed-light, up to 10,000 sq ft) or $2,000 (indoor, up to 10,000 sq ft); qualified social-and-economic-equity applicants pay half. Nursery holders may sell to cultivators, microbusinesses and other nurseries — not to retailers or consumers — and applicants may not hold interests in retail, delivery or on-site consumption businesses.

Why it matters: A new supply-side tier with strict retail separation — New York partners with any dispensary interest are ineligible, and the 50-application cap makes timing decisive.

Missouri study finds licensed market meeting demand, sees no need for more licenses

MISSOURI • Marijuana Moment • Oct 24

A study for Missouri regulators by Cannabis Public Policy Consulting found licensed dispensaries capture about 93% of consumer purchases and concluded the regulated market is "effectively meeting demand," with license counts — 222 dispensary, 85 cultivation and 63 manufacturing licenses approved — keeping pace with roughly $1.5 billion in annual sales. Regulators filed rules this month that would require licensees to report ownership percentages annually, and Division Director Amy Moore said future studies will add ownership-concentration data.

Why it matters: Missouri license counts are likely to stay flat, and the proposed annual ownership report would add a new compliance filing for every license holder.

On the Radar

Oct. 29 / Nov. 5 — DEA Chief ALJ Derek Julius admitted the GAO drug-scheduling report into the marijuana rescheduling record and set briefing on its weight for Oct. 29, replies by Nov. 5; the case stay expires when briefing closes (Marijuana Moment).

Nov. 12 — The federal ban on synthetic, lab-made cannabinoids in hemp products takes effect; this date was not moved by the December delay (Vicente LLP).

Dec. 11 — The federal total-THC standard (0.3% total THC) and the 0.4 mg total-THC-per-container cap take effect for hemp products, and UPS's certificate-of-analysis requirement for hemp shipments begins the same day.
