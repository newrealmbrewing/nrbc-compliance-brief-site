Top Story

Massachusetts senators say they will vote against ballot measure ending retail cannabis sales

MASSACHUSETTS • Marijuana Moment • Oct 8

Sens. Elizabeth Warren and Ed Markey said they will vote against Question 8, the November 3 ballot measure that would repeal the laws allowing licensed commercial sales of adult-use cannabis in Massachusetts; legal possession and the medical program would continue. Warren said "legalizing cannabis is better than continuing an unregulated, underground market," while the measure's campaign, largely funded by SAM Action, has focused its advertising on the dangers of marijuana-impaired driving. Recent polls show the measure trailing — 61% of likely voters opposed in a MassINC survey and 71% in a University of New Hampshire poll.

Why it matters: A yes vote on November 3 would close every licensed adult-use retail outlet in Massachusetts — a structural question for any supplier, distributor, or retail partner with cannabis-adjacent business in the state, and a test case other states will watch.

Around the States

Washington regulators open rulemaking to help licensees document medical-market status for federal registration

WASHINGTON • Marijuana Moment • Oct 8

The Liquor and Cannabis Board voted 3-0 to accept a rulemaking petition — over a staff recommendation to reject it — to create a process for cannabis businesses to request state verification of activities authorized under medical cannabis laws, usable for federal registration, banking, insurance, and other purposes. Staff said verification would rest on licensee self-reporting and could carry significant administrative burden; board member Pete Holmes called those concerns "very real" but backed the petition.

Why it matters: Washington's June guidance said federal Schedule III rescheduling benefits may not reach its licensees because the state doesn't separately license medical operators — this rulemaking is the state's first step toward documentation DEA registration requires.

Missouri jury awards $10 million in cannabis license ownership dispute as state drafts tighter ownership rules

MISSOURI • Marijuana Moment / Missouri Independent • Oct 8

A St. Louis jury awarded $10 million on breach-of-contract and tortious-interference claims in a fight over six Missouri licenses, including a Waynesville cultivation facility and stakes in four dispensaries. Separately, state regulators have proposed rules — publishing November 2, with comments through December 2 — allowing fines up to $100,000 or license suspension when a person with past violations exercises "controlling influence" over a facility, and replacing some pre-approvals with annual ownership reports.

Why it matters: Ownership-change compliance is becoming an enforcement focus — partners with Missouri license interests should watch the November 2 rule text and the December 2 comment deadline.

Minnesota retailer Top Ten Liquors says it will keep stocking THC beverages ahead of federal deadline

MINNESOTA • Brewbound • Oct 7

Top Ten Liquors owner and CEO Jon Halper said the Minnesota chain plans to keep selling THC products as the December 11 federal limits approach, writing that "we plan to keep supporting the brands that have invested in this category." Retailers nationally are weighing sell-through timing for hemp-THC inventory against the federal date and their own states' rules.

Sierra Nevada terminates Coors Distributing Co., moves Denver-area rights to Reyes

COLORADO • Brewbound • Oct 7

Sierra Nevada is ending its relationship with Molson Coors-owned Coors Distributing Company in the Denver area and moving to Reyes Beer Division, joining what Brewbound describes as a growing list of suppliers exiting CDC's Colorado portfolio.

Why it matters: Brand-rights consolidation toward the largest wholesaler networks continues — distributors in overlapping territories should expect further portfolio movement and the franchise-law filings that come with it.

On the Radar

Dec. 11 — Federal limits on intoxicating hemp products (0.4 mg total THC per container) take effect unless Congress acts; the ban on chemically synthesized cannabinoids arrives earlier, on Nov. 12, which is also the effective date of Illinois's Hemp Act (Foley Hoag).

Nov. 13 — New Jersey prohibits sales of intoxicating hemp beverages outside the cannabis-license system; until then, ABC plenary wholesale and plenary retail distribution licensees and Class 5 cannabis retailers may sell compliant beverages (5 mg THC/serving, 10 mg/container) (NJ CRC).

Nov. 3 — Election Day: Massachusetts votes on Question 8 (today's top story); Idaho votes on a constitutional amendment reserving drug-legalization decisions to the legislature.
