Top Story

North Carolina businesses race to clear THC drink inventory before the Dec. 11 federal deadline

NORTH CAROLINA • WBTV • Nov 26

North Carolina hemp businesses are working to move THC beverage inventory before December 11, when the revised federal hemp definition takes effect; under it, finished products with more than 0.4 milligrams of combined total THC and similar-effect cannabinoids per container no longer count as hemp under federal law, per a Congressional Research Service summary cited by WBTV. Charlotte's NoDa Brewing, which says its Happy Bird THC seltzer accounts for about 10% of revenue, ran buy-one-get-one discounts this week to clear stock, and retailer Apotheca estimates the change would affect about 98% of its current inventory. A January economic analysis cited in the report put North Carolina's hemp-derived cannabinoid industry at $3.2 billion in business revenue and more than 16,000 workers.

Why it matters: Distributors and retailers holding hemp-THC beverages face the same two-week clock — product still on hand after Dec. 11 may no longer qualify as hemp under federal law, so sell-down, return and quarantine decisions need to be settled now, state by state.

Federal

Rescheduling opponents ask D.C. Circuit to overturn the Schedule III move

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 25

Smart Approaches to Marijuana, the National Drug and Alcohol Screening Association, the attorneys general of Indiana and Nebraska, and other challengers filed a brief arguing the administration's reclassification of state-licensed medical marijuana to Schedule III was, in their words, "audacious agency overreach" that skipped the formal rulemaking process Congress mandated; the Justice Department has defended the orders. Briefing in the consolidated cases is set to finish by mid-February, with oral argument expected before the court's summer recess.

Why it matters: The ruling will decide whether Schedule III status — and the federal 280E tax relief that came with it — stands for state-licensed medical cannabis businesses, a planning variable for any partner with exposure to that market.

Around the States

South Carolina senator asks Congress to delay the hemp restrictions, back safeguards instead

SOUTH CAROLINA • ABC News 4 (WCIV) • Nov 24

State Sen. Deon Tedder (D-Charleston) sent a letter to South Carolina's congressional delegation urging Congress to delay or block the federal hemp restrictions taking effect Dec. 11, calling instead for what he described as "commonsense regulation, not prohibition" — minimum age limits, independent product testing, clear labeling, THC limits, child-resistant packaging and enforcement. A state-level restriction bill died in the legislature over the summer.

Rhode Island regulators publish updated hemp guidance and FAQ ahead of the federal deadline

RHODE ISLAND • Marijuana Moment • Nov 27

Rhode Island regulators released updated hemp guidance and a new FAQ as the Dec. 11 federal change approaches. The state's Cannabis Control Commission has told its roughly 200 hemp licensees they may quarantine over-limit inventory rather than destroy it, with inventory reports due to the commission by Dec. 4.

Why it matters: Rhode Island's quarantine-not-destroy approach is one model other state regulators are watching; partners with Rhode Island operations have a Dec. 4 filing deadline.

On the Radar

Dec. 11 — The revised federal hemp definition takes effect: finished hemp-derived products with more than 0.4 mg of combined total THC and similar-effect cannabinoids per container no longer qualify as hemp under federal law.

Dec. 4 — Rhode Island hemp licensees' inventory reports are due to the state Cannabis Control Commission under its quarantine guidance.

Dec. 1 / Jan. 13 — In the D.C. Circuit rescheduling litigation, amicus briefs supporting the challengers are due Dec. 1 and the government's response Jan. 13, with briefing complete by mid-February.
