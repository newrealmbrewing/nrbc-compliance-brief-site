Top Story

Griffith proposes putting hemp THC rules in a post-election reconciliation bill

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Oct 27

Rep. Morgan Griffith (R-VA) told Roll Call he wants the HEMP Act — the bill he co-filed with Rep. Marc Veasey (D-TX) to keep hemp products legal under new market rules — added to a budget reconciliation bill in the lame-duck session, before the federal limit of 0.4 milligrams of total THC per container takes effect for most hemp products on December 11. Griffith suggested pairing it with a revenue measure such as Rep. Beth Van Duyne’s (R-TX) bill to tax and regulate hemp THC drinks like alcohol, saying “if we don’t do something before that December 11 deadline, it’s going to create all kinds of issues.” Reconciliation can pass with a simple Senate majority, though provisions not directly tied to spending or revenue can be challenged and removed; Griffith also noted some colleagues want intoxicating hemp products stopped outright.

Why it matters: Whether hemp THC beverages remain sellable after Dec. 11 now rides on an uncertain lame-duck vehicle — distributors and retailers should plan Q4 inventory and promotions around the Dec. 11 limit as written, not a possible fix.

Around the States

Rhode Island details hemp quarantine terms: inventory reports due Dec. 4, no sales after Dec. 11

RHODE ISLAND • Marijuana Moment / Rhode Island Current • Oct 27

Following up on the quarantine option we covered Oct. 22: Cannabis Control Commission guidance to the state’s 201 hemp licensees confirms that inventory over the federal 0.4 mg total-THC-per-container limit may be held after December 11 but cannot be processed, moved, distributed or sold without federal or commission authorization. Licensees must submit inventory reports by December 4, destruction requests must be supervised by the Cannabis Office, and commission spokesperson Charon Rose said the earlier remove-or-destroy directive was replaced to avoid forcing destruction while Congress may still change the federal rules.

Why it matters: Rhode Island retail partners holding hemp beverages face a hard Dec. 4 paperwork deadline and an effective sell-through date of Dec. 10.

South Dakota finalizes 35 medical marijuana rule changes, effective Nov. 5

SOUTH DAKOTA • The Marijuana Herald • Oct 27

Department of Health amendments published in the October 26 state register revise penalties, testing, security and surveillance rules for the medical marijuana program. First-time Category 4 and Category 5 violations now get a corrective-action-plan option instead of an immediate fine ($1,000 and $500 fines apply from the second offense; Category 1 fines remain $5,000–$10,000), and certificates of analysis must disclose decontamination or pretreatment.

On the Radar

Nov. 12 / Dec. 11 — The federal recriminalization of synthetic cannabinoids and Illinois’s Hemp Act licensing regime take effect Nov. 12; the federal 0.4 mg total-THC-per-container limit for most hemp products takes effect Dec. 11 unless Congress acts first (see today’s top story).

Supreme Court — The justices have agreed to review the 1868 federal ban on distilling spirits at home, taking up a Fifth Circuit ruling that the ban exceeds Congress’s taxing power; the outcome could clarify the constitutional basis of federal excise-tax controls on alcohol production. A decision is expected this term.

Nov. 3 — Election Day: Massachusetts votes on Question 8, which would repeal commercial cannabis sales while keeping possession and the medical program. In New Jersey, S4635, which would extend licensed hemp-beverage sales from Nov. 13 to at least Dec. 11, passed the Senate 37–0 and awaits an Assembly floor vote.
