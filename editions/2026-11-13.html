Good morning — here's the regulatory picture for alcohol and hemp/THC beverages, readable in about three minutes. Every headline links to the original source.

TOP STORY

First phase of the federal hemp ban takes effect; synthetic cannabinoids now prohibited

FEDERAL • Cannabis Business Times • Nov 12

The first phase of last year's federal hemp definition change took effect Wednesday, November 12: hemp products containing cannabinoids "not capable of being naturally produced by a Cannabis sativa L. plant" — such as THC-O — are now federally prohibited, while naturally occurring cannabinoids such as delta-8 and delta-10 THC remain lawful until the second phase on December 11, which bars products with more than 0.4 milligrams of total THC per container. The FDA has not yet published the cannabinoid lists or the definition of "container" that the law required by February 10 — a gap hemp attorney Rod Kight said leaves sellers with "significant federal legal exposure" to weigh. The change landed the same day new state regimes took hold in Missouri and Illinois (below).

Why it matters: Any product containing synthesized or converted cannabinoids is now federally non-compliant regardless of state law, and December 11 remains the operative deadline for standard hemp THC beverages — inventory sell-through and return plans should be set against that date.

FEDERAL

Griffith aims to attach hemp regulation to a lame-duck reconciliation bill before December 11

FEDERAL • Cannabis Business Times • Oct 27

Rep. Morgan Griffith (R-Va.) plans to attach the HEMP Act — the framework he introduced with Rep. Marc Veasey (D-Texas) that would allow sales to adults 21 and over at up to 5 mg of THC per serving and 30 mg per package — to a filibuster-proof reconciliation package in the lame-duck session; this week he said that if Congress does not act before December 11, "it's going to create all kinds of issues." A separate bill from Reps. Beth Van Duyne (R-Texas) and Greg Landsman (D-Ohio), the Beverage Regulatory Parity Act, would route hemp beverages of up to 5 mg per serving through a three-tier system. The U.S. Cannabis Roundtable and the American Trade Association for Cannabis and Hemp have lobbied lawmakers against replacing the ban with regulation; ATACH's Chris Lindsey said the current framework "empowers bad actors, threatens public health and undermines state regulations."

Why it matters: Whether a regulatory framework displaces the December 11 ban decides if hemp THC beverages remain a lawful retail category into 2027 — the reconciliation vehicle is the near-term path to watch.

AROUND THE STATES

Missouri's intoxicating hemp products move to dispensary-only sales

MISSOURI • Cannabis Business Times • Nov 12

Missouri's HB 2641, signed in April, took effect November 12, requiring most hemp-derived intoxicating products to be sold through licensed cannabis dispensaries under Department of Health and Senior Services oversight; products above 0.4 mg of total THC per container are reclassified into the state's marijuana framework. Sponsor Rep. Dave Hinman said the state is "just following the federal government's new law."

Why it matters: Missouri grocery, convenience and liquor accounts can no longer carry intoxicating hemp products — distributors with Missouri inventory outside the dispensary channel need reroute or return plans now.

Illinois hemp farms and CBD retailers wind down under the state's new THC limits

ILLINOIS • Shaw Local • Nov 12

Northern Illinois hemp farms and CBD retailers are cutting product lines or closing as the state's new THC limits take hold: many full-spectrum CBD products exceed the new cap, and dispensaries must now source CBD oil from licensed growers rather than hemp farms. Grower Amanda Montgomery said a "$5,000 license to only sell 10% of my product" is not viable for small farms, and McHenry retailer CannaBliss is closing with 75% markdowns; owner Scott Giles noted that "CBD itself is not being completely banned."

Why it matters: The contraction now reaches non-intoxicating CBD assortments, not just THC beverages — Illinois retail partners carrying full-spectrum CBD face the same compliance dates.

New York raises the wholesale cider add-on fee for beer wholesalers to a one-time $375

NEW YORK • N.Y. State Senate (Ch. 332, A.11437) • Oct 23

License fee change: Chapter 332 of 2026 (A.11437), signed October 23 and effective immediately, amends ABC Law §58-a to raise the additional fee a licensed wholesale beer licensee pays to also sell cider at wholesale from $125 to $375 per licensee — and converts it from an annual fee to a one-time fee, with the cider authorization running concurrently with the underlying wholesale beer license. The act changes only this wholesaler add-on; it does not alter manufacturer or retailer license fees.

Why it matters: For New York distribution partners adding cider to a beer book, licensing cost is now a flat $375 once instead of $125 every year — more upfront, cheaper from year four, and one fewer renewal to track.

ON THE RADAR

Nov. 19 — U.S. District Court hearing in Chicago on the Illinois hemp industry's restraining-order and preliminary-injunction motions; the state's enforcement pause for the ~250 lawsuit businesses expires the same day (CBS Chicago).

Dec. 11 — second phase of the federal hemp definition change: products above 0.4 mg of total THC (including THCA) per container lose federal hemp status nationwide (Cannabis Business Times).

Rescheduling — the DEA and rescheduling opponents have filed what are expected to be their final briefs in the marijuana rescheduling proceeding; the agency judge's recommendation is the next step (Marijuana Moment).
