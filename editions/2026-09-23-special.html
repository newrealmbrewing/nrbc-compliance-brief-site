Top Story

Virginia drafts its adult-use retail rules: tiered fee schedule, 350-store cap, July 2027 launch

VIRGINIA • Virginia Business • Sep 11

The Virginia Cannabis Control Authority released an 82-page draft of the regulations that will govern the state’s adult-use retail marijuana market on September 9, covering license types and caps, fees, delivery, purchase limits and store siting. State law caps the market at 350 retail stores and five Tier V cultivation facilities (35,000-square-foot canopy); the CCA is still determining other operational caps. “These are not final by any means,” said Jake Shuford, the CCA’s legislative and regulatory manager. The authority plans to finalize the rules in December, open applications by February 1, 2027 (first phase: microbusinesses, hemp processors, cultivators and testing labs — retail applications follow), issue initial licenses by May 1, 2027, and launch retail sales July 1, 2027. Licensees have 18 months to become operational or forfeit the license. Stakeholder input is open now through the CCA’s Regulations Input page, with the authority expecting preliminary feedback by late October (Axios Richmond, Richmond BizSense).

Why it matters: For a retail partner, the draft puts the cost of opening one Virginia store at $24,000 up front ($4,000 application + $20,000 authorization) plus $15,000 a year thereafter, per location — and with only 350 store licenses statewide and the first application window opening February 1, 2027, partners weighing entry have roughly four months to shape the rules and position before the field is set.

The Draft Fee Schedule

Retail stores (up to 350 statewide) — $4,000 application • $20,000 initial authorization • $15,000 annual renewal, per store.

Microbusinesses (up to 100) — $1,000 application • $2,500 initial authorization • $6,000 annual renewal.

Cultivation (five tiers by canopy size) — $3,000–$7,500 application • $20,000–$50,000 first year • $15,000–$45,000 annual renewal; only five Tier V (largest-canopy) licenses are initially available.

Existing medical operators — medical processors entering the adult-use market pay a $10 million dual-use fee, per Axios Richmond; Virginia Business separately reports a $40,000 initial permit for cannabis dispensing facilities.

Impact applicants — owners with a 51%+ stake living in disproportionately policed or economically disadvantaged areas may request reduced or waived fees, and may share equipment and facilities with microbusinesses through cooperative agreements.

Figures are from the draft as reported September 11–21 by Virginia Business, Richmond BizSense and Axios Richmond; all remain subject to change before final adoption in December.

Operating Rules in the Draft

Home delivery would be authorized, while drive-thru windows and curbside pickup are prohibited. Purchases are capped at 2 ounces of flower per transaction, or the equivalent — 8 grams of concentrate or 800 mg of THC in edibles. Stores must sit at least 1,000 feet from schools and hospitals, and home-based businesses are excluded. Where application volume exceeds available licenses, the CCA anticipates lottery selection.

Why it matters: The delivery authorization opens a fulfillment channel for retail partners, while the no-drive-thru/no-curbside rule and the 1,000-foot setback constrain site selection — real-estate decisions made before December’s final rules carry regulatory risk.

Key Dates

Late October 2026 — The CCA expects preliminary stakeholder feedback; the 2026 Regulation Input Survey is open now.

December 2026 — Final regulations expected.

February 1, 2027 — Applications open (first phase: microbusiness, hemp processor, cultivation and testing-lab licenses; retail follows). Initial licenses are due to be issued by May 1, 2027.

July 1, 2027 — Retail sales launch; licensees then have 18 months from licensure to become operational.
