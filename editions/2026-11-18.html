Top Story

Sheehy and Klobuchar file Hemp Guardrails Act to replace the December 11 hemp ban with federal regulation

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 17

Sens. Tim Sheehy (R-MT) and Amy Klobuchar (D-MN) introduced the Hemp Guardrails Act, the Senate proposal advocates hope to attach to must-pass legislation before the federal hemp THC ban takes effect December 11. The bill would raise the hemp threshold to 1 percent total THC on a dry-weight basis, set a 21-plus minimum age, restrict delta-8, delta-10 and THC-O made outside the plant, require U.S. growing and packaging, and direct FDA to issue packaging, labeling, testing and recall rules within 180 days; states could keep stricter rules but could not block interstate transport of compliant products. Sheehy called it “a commonsense approach,” and Klobuchar said the looming ban threatens farmers, brewers and small businesses.

Why it matters: Unless Congress acts, hemp beverages over 0.4 mg total THC per container become federally illegal on December 11 — this bill is the Senate vehicle, and its progress over the next three weeks is the clearest signal for distributor and retail ordering decisions.

Federal

Comer says a hemp fix’s best chance is riding the next continuing resolution

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 17

Rep. James Comer (R-KY) said the best opportunity for hemp provisions is “to get this on a bill that is going to pass,” pointing to the next continuing resolution, while Rep. Morgan Griffith (R-VA) has floated a budget reconciliation bill as an alternative vehicle. The article also notes a Congressional Research Service finding that FDA is more than nine months late on a legally required list of known cannabinoids and container guidance.

VFW-led veterans coalition asks Congress to regulate hemp THC products rather than ban them

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 17

A coalition led by the Veterans of Foreign Wars — joined by the Non Commissioned Officers Association, National American Indian Veterans, Burn Pits 360 and others — sent House and Senate leaders a letter asking for a federal framework with a 21-plus purchase age, mandatory third-party testing, child-resistant packaging and agency oversight, writing that “a federal framework can do what a ban cannot.”

Around the States

California ABC posts 2027 fee schedule: every application and annual license fee rises 3.31% on January 1

CALIFORNIA • California ABC (official) • Posted for Jan 1, 2027

The Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control’s posted schedule raises all application and annual license fees 3.31 percent over 2026 amounts effective January 1, 2027, rounded to the nearest five dollars, for every ABC license holder. Current annual fees that will adjust include: beer manufacturer (Type 01, over 60,000 barrels) $2,450; small beer manufacturer (Type 23) $500; beer and wine wholesaler (Type 17) $500; off-sale beer and wine (Type 20) $500; off-sale general (Type 21) $1,009; and on-sale general $985–$1,545 depending on city population. Fees are annual and per license; the full schedule lists every type, and late renewals draw a 50 percent penalty within 60 days and 100 percent after.

Why it matters: California retail and distribution partners should budget the higher renewal at each licensed location — a multi-store chain pays the increase once per license, and the percentage applies across every tier from manufacturer to retailer.

Ohio committee weighs franchise rewrite letting small producers end distributor agreements without cause

OHIO • Libation Law Blog • Nov 16

A substitute version of H.B. 194, in the Ohio House Judiciary Committee’s November 10 hearing record, would let producers of 25,000 or fewer barrels a year of beer or wine terminate distribution franchises “for any reason or no reason” on 45 days’ notice — current law requires just cause and 60 days — with fair-market-value compensation to the wholesaler when the agreement sets no formula. The original bill applied only to beer and used a 250,000-barrel threshold; the bill remains in committee.

Why it matters: Ohio wholesalers could lose small-producer brands without cause once an agreement is renewed after the effective date, and would have 45 days post-termination to dispute compensation.

On the Radar

December 11: The federal hemp THC ban (0.4 mg total THC per container) and the current government funding stopgap expire the same day — making the next funding bill the likeliest vehicle for any hemp fix.

DEA rescheduling: Final briefs are in at DEA’s marijuana rescheduling hearing; the administrative decision is pending with no announced date.

FDA hemp guidance: Per a Congressional Research Service report, FDA remains more than nine months late on the legally required cannabinoid list and container guidance that would underpin any new hemp framework.
