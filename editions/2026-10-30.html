Top Story

Senators ask USPS to stay out of Georgia's medical cannabis delivery plan

FEDERAL / GEORGIA • Marijuana Moment • Oct 29

Sens. James Lankford (R-OK) and Ted Budd (R-NC) sent a letter to Postmaster General David Steiner urging USPS to keep its prohibition on mailing marijuana, after the Georgia Access to Medical Cannabis Commission voted last month to allow medical cannabis deliveries through USPS, FedEx, UPS and DHL. The senators wrote that they “strongly oppose any effort to allow the shipment of marijuana” and said Georgia lacks authority to change federal postal rules; commission Executive Director Andrew Turnage said the rule “provides a great pathway.” A USPS spokesperson pointed to existing rules on mailing controlled substances and said the agency had “nothing additional to offer at this time”; UPS and FedEx did not comment, and DHL said it will “continue to comply with all applicable laws.”

Why it matters: Carrier participation decides whether home delivery becomes a real purchasing channel for THC products in Georgia — a shift that would route sales around traditional retail shelves that Georgia retailers and distributors should watch.

Federal

House bill would rewrite the Controlled Substances Act and deschedule marijuana

U.S. HOUSE • Marijuana Moment • Oct 29

Rep. Steve Cohen (D-TN) filed the Public Health and Research Reforms In Controlled Substances Act, which would replace the five-schedule system with a dual-axis framework rating abuse potential and medical efficacy separately, shift core scheduling authority from the Justice Department and DEA to HHS, and remove marijuana from the act entirely. Cohen, who is retiring at the end of this year, said “a thoughtful review of national drug policy is long overdue”; the bill has no scheduled committee action.

Around the States

Michigan bill would pause new 24% wholesale marijuana tax for one year

MICHIGAN • Marijuana Moment • Oct 29

Rep. Jay DeBoyer (R) filed HB 6440 to suspend for one year the 24 percent wholesale cannabis tax enacted this year to fund road repairs, saying it is “generating tens of millions of dollars less in revenue than what was projected.” Michigan Cannabis Industry Association director Robin Schneider said a 12-month pause “will give what's left of our cannabis businesses time to stabilize”; the bill is pending with no committee action yet.

Why it matters: A 24% wholesale tax flows straight into shelf prices — a suspension would change margins quickly for Michigan retailers and the distributors supplying them.

Spending on Massachusetts marijuana-repeal ballot fight nears $6.5 million

MASSACHUSETTS • Marijuana Moment / CommonWealth Beacon • Oct 29

Campaigns for and against Question 8 — which would eliminate the state's recreational marijuana industry — had spent nearly $6.5 million through Oct. 15, with repeal supporters at more than $3.7 million, almost all from SAM Action Inc., and opponents at more than $2.8 million. Voters decide Tuesday, Nov. 3.

Why it matters: A yes vote would close the licensed recreational retail channel in Massachusetts — the outcome lands next week and resets planning for anyone selling into that market.

On the Radar

Nov. 12 — Products with cannabinoids that the cannabis plant cannot naturally produce are excluded from the federal hemp definition and regulated as marijuana; Illinois' new hemp retail rules take effect the same day.

Dec. 4 — Rhode Island deadline for hemp businesses to file inventory reports with state regulators.

Dec. 11 — The federal limit of 0.4 mg of total THC per consumer container takes effect under the 2025 farm-bill rider — the compliance date that defines the future of most hemp THC beverages in their current form unless Congress acts first.
