Top Story

Maryland drafts wide-ranging cannabis rule changes, tying license renewals to tax compliance

MARYLAND • Md. Cannabis Administration • Sep 22

The Maryland Cannabis Administration has posted draft amendments to its cannabis regulations (COMAR 14.17, draft dated September 16) covering license renewals, ownership transfers, advertising, employee training, packaging and product sales. Under the draft, the MCA could deny a license renewal if the licensee has taxes in arrears in any jurisdiction; ownership-transfer reviews of criminal history and financial information would be codified; a Cannabis Agent Training program would replace Responsible Vendor Training; allergens would have to be listed on the outer marketing layer; and micro dispensaries could employ up to 20 people, up from 10. On fees: the draft adds a new $1,000 per-petition filing fee for declaratory rulings, payable by any licensee or applicant requesting one — it does not change the annual license fees for the grower, processor or dispensary tiers. Informal comments are due to the MCA by September 30; a formal 30-day comment period follows publication in the Maryland Register.

Why it matters: For any partner holding or acquiring a Maryland cannabis license, tax compliance in every jurisdiction where they operate would become a renewal condition, ownership changes would face a codified state review, and each formal interpretive request would carry a new $1,000 cost — the September 30 informal window is the cheapest chance to shape the final text.

Federal

Two-thirds of Missouri cannabis operators skipped DEA registration under rescheduling — and none who applied are approved yet

FEDERAL / MISSOURI • MJBizDaily • Sep 22

After April’s federal rescheduling, DEA opened a registration portal tied to potential Section 280E tax relief, but roughly two-thirds of Missouri operators declined to apply, citing ownership- and employee-disclosure requirements, and none of the one-third that did apply had been approved as of Monday, according to the Missouri Cannabis Trade Association’s Andrew Mullins. Attorney Marshall Custer of Husch Blackwell said operators who skipped registration “made a mistake” because registration is a requirement for Schedule III advantages; Mullins said he reads rescheduling as extending 280E relief to medical sales regardless of registration, and attorney Eric Walter said the framework “was written for MMJ-only states.”

Why it matters: Whether 280E relief requires DEA registration is unresolved and Treasury has not issued guidance — a material federal-tax variable for any partner with cannabis-license exposure, and a rollout signal worth tracking as other combined medical/adult-use states face the same choice.

Around the States

Minnesota liquor retailers report THC beverage growth heading into December federal deadline

MINNESOTA • Shanken News Daily • Sep 22

Top Ten Liquors CEO Jon Halper said THC beverages are up 12% year-to-date through August and now rank as the chain’s fourth-largest department at roughly 15% of sales, with the THC set expanded from 500 to 750 items and 12-pack sales up 150% year over year — “the category has moved from trial to stock-up,” he said. Surdyk’s CEO Melissa Surdyk called Minnesota “a strong market,” crediting improved consumer education. The growth comes as the federal hemp redefinition remains set to take effect December 11.

Why it matters: Minnesota’s liquor-store channel shows hemp-beverage demand shifting to larger pack sizes and repeat purchase — but Q4 inventory commitments in the category carry regulatory risk unless Congress acts again before the December 11 federal effective date.

On the Radar

September 29 — The presidential order excluding Canadian wine, spirits and beer from U.S. importation takes effect; product imported earlier but not yet entered for consumption stays subject to the existing 50% duty (Shanken News Daily).

October 29 — Written comments are due to the U.S. Trade Representative for the 2027 National Trade Estimate report identifying foreign barriers to U.S. exports, including alcohol; TTB flagged the docket for its regulated industries (TTB Newsletter).

December 11 — The federal hemp redefinition limiting legal products to 0.4 mg total THC per container takes effect, after Congress moved the date back from November 12 in the funding bill signed September 4 (Marijuana Moment).
