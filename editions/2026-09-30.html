Top Story

Connecticut's flat 10.75% cannabis tax and higher THC beverage limits take effect tomorrow

CONNECTICUT • CT Mirror / Shipman & Goodwin • effective Oct 1

On Thursday, October 1, Connecticut replaces its potency-based cannabis excise taxes (previously 0.625¢/mg for flower, 2.75¢/mg for edibles, 0.9¢/mg for other products) with a flat 10.75% tax on gross receipts — a combined register rate of about 20.1% once the 6.35% state sales tax and 3% municipal tax are added. Under Public Acts 26-8 and 26-68, the same date brings higher limits for THC-infused beverages — up to 5 mg total THC per container in package stores and 10 mg in cannabis retailers, versus the prior 3 mg — plus recognition of out-of-state medical cannabis cards and elimination of the 70% potency cap on concentrates. The state's Office of Fiscal Analysis projects the tax change will reduce annual revenue by roughly $2 million.

Why it matters: Connecticut package stores can stock stronger 5 mg THC beverages starting tomorrow — a meaningful expansion of the liquor-retail channel — and every cannabis retailer in the state must re-price to the new gross-receipts tax basis at the register on day one.

Federal

TTB: third-quarter CBMA import refund claims open Thursday, October 1

FEDERAL • TTB Newsletter • Sep 25

In its latest newsletter, TTB reminded importers that third-quarter Craft Beverage Modernization Act (CBMA) refund claims may be filed starting Thursday, October 1, flagged three excise tax return periods falling in September, and announced an upcoming webinar on TTB export certificates. Why it matters: importers claiming reduced CBMA rates can start recovering Q3 excise overpayments this week — a cash-flow item worth calendaring.

Around the States

Coors Distributing sues Constellation and Mark Anthony in civil court to block terminations

COLORADO • Brewbound • Sep 29

Molson Coors–owned Coors Distributing Company filed civil lawsuits Tuesday seeking to halt brand terminations by Constellation Brands and Mark Anthony Brands, two of its largest suppliers — escalating the dispute beyond the administrative challenges it lodged with Colorado regulators last week.

Why it matters: the case will test how far Colorado's distributor-termination protections reach — and its outcome determines who services major import and RTD brands for Denver-area retail accounts.

California enacts cannabis testing and track-and-trace bills; bars tobacco retailers from cannabis sales

CALIFORNIA • Marijuana Moment • Sep 29

Gov. Gavin Newsom signed AB 1965, which mandates testing and retesting of cannabis products, lets the Department of Cannabis Control pull products for independent lab checks, and requires retailers to provide certificates of analysis on consumer request; and AB 2250, which expands track-and-trace across the supply chain and prohibits licensed tobacco and cigarette retailers from selling cannabis. Why it matters: California retailers face new certificate-of-analysis obligations, and the tobacco/convenience channel is expressly shut out of cannabis sales.

On the Radar

December 11, 2026 — the federal hemp ban's delayed effective date. H.R. 6500, signed September 2, pushed most restrictions back from November 12; the ban on synthetic cannabinoids still takes effect November 12, 2026. Absent further congressional action, hemp-derived THC beverages lose their federal lawful status on December 11.

Late October — the Virginia Cannabis Control Authority's preliminary-feedback window on its draft adult-use retail regulations approaches, with final rules due in December and license applications opening February 1, 2027 (covered in full in our September 23 special edition).

December 1, 2026 — Connecticut infused-beverage manufacturers may begin incorporating intermediate hemp derivatives into products, a second phase of the state's 2026 cannabis and hemp law.
