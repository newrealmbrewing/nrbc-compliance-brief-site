Top Story

Montana ties cannabis licenses to resident operational control, effective today

MONTANA • Cannabis Business Times • Sep 24

The Montana Department of Revenue adopted a rule, effective September 26, requiring every cannabis licensee — across cultivation, manufacturing, testing, transport and retail license types — to identify each individual with "day-to-day operational control" of the business and provide proof of Montana residency for those individuals. The definition reaches anyone who directs or materially influences routine management, operations or policies, including general managers and store managers with contract or spending authority. The department cited the Ninth Circuit's 2026 Peridot Tree decision, which held that dormant Commerce Clause protections do not apply to state cannabis licensing residency rules while cannabis remains federally scheduled, and said the state's marijuana law "has always contained a residency requirement"; Pepper Petersen of the Coldwater Group said the requirement violates the dormant Commerce Clause and burdens small operators.

Why it matters: This is a licensing-eligibility change touching every license tier in the state — any operator or partner with out-of-state managers or multistate ownership in Montana needs resident operational control documented now, since the rule applies to applications and renewals starting today.

Around the States

Arizona attorney general sues Feel Free maker Botanic Tonics under consumer fraud law

ARIZONA • Brewbound • Sep 25

Attorney General Kris Mayes filed what her office called a first-in-the-nation consumer protection lawsuit against Botanic Tonics, alleging the company marketed its 2-ounce Feel Free kratom-and-kava shots as energy drinks and wellness products while concealing the kratom content and its addiction and withdrawal risks, and used subscriptions, discounts and college-focused advertising to drive repeat purchases. Mayes said the company "repeatedly and willfully violated the Arizona Consumer Fraud Act"; state health officials counted 146 kratom-related deaths from 2020 to 2023.

Why it matters: The disclosure-and-marketing theory behind this suit reaches the functional-shot shelf set that sits beside hemp THC beverages in many accounts — retailers and distributors carrying kratom, kava or similar SKUs in Arizona should review labeling claims and any risk-disclosure gaps with suppliers.

Pennsylvania Senate panel takes up adult-use legalization as two bills sit pending

PENNSYLVANIA • Cannabis Business Times • Sep 25

The Senate Democratic Policy Committee, chaired by Sens. Nick Miller and Sharif Street, held a Philadelphia hearing September 25 on the criminal justice, safety and economic impacts of adult-use cannabis. Two bills are pending: SB 49, which would create a Cannabis Control Board and stalled over the summer, and Street's SB 120 legalization bill, now calendared. The hearing followed Attorney General Dave Sunday's announcement of charges against 12 people in an alleged $18.7 million illegal marijuana operation; ResponsiblePA's Monica McCafferty said intoxicating products are already "masquerading as legal hemp" in the state and argued a regulated market would displace them.

Why it matters: Pennsylvania is one of the largest remaining prohibition states; any legalization framework that emerges will set the licensing and shelf rules for THC products — including beverages now sold under hemp rules — in a market that matters to every regional distributor.

On the Radar

September 29 — The presidential order excluding most Canadian packaged wine, spirits and beer from U.S. importation takes effect at 12:01 a.m. ET; product imported earlier but not yet entered for consumption stays subject to the existing 50% duty (Shanken News Daily). In-transit Canadian SKUs must be entered before the deadline.

Late October — The Virginia Cannabis Control Authority's preliminary-feedback window on its draft adult-use retail regulations is expected; final rules are due in December, license applications open February 1, 2027, and retail sales launch July 1, 2027 (Virginia CCA).

November 12 / December 11 — Missouri's state restrictions on hemp THC products take effect November 12, and the federal hemp redefinition limiting legal products to 0.4 mg total THC per container takes effect December 11 (Marijuana Moment).
