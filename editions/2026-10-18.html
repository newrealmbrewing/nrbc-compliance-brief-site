Top Story

Kentucky hemp industry asks lawmakers to cut license fees and restore on-premises hemp beverage sales ahead of the 2027 session

KENTUCKY • Spectrum News 1 • Oct 15

Kentucky Hemp Association members told the legislature's interim joint Licensing, Occupations and Administrative Regulations Committee in Frankfort that the state should lower hemp licensing fees, restore on-premises sales of hemp beverages and ban open containers while driving; the association says the industry employs more than 3,000 people. Committee chair Rep. Matthew Koch (R-Paris) said testing and accurate THC beverage labeling are his priorities, comparing the oversight he wants to the bourbon industry's. License fees & structure: Annie Rouse, owner of manufacturer-retailer Cannabuzz, told lawmakers she pays $13,780 a year in combined Kentucky licensing fees to manufacture and sell hemp products while a large distillery with equivalent operations pays $3,990 (micro-distilleries about $2,000); she asked lawmakers to eliminate duplicate licenses, combine the manufacturing licenses into one, and cut the hemp shipper license to $100 to match the spirits equivalent. These are requests, not enacted changes — any new fee schedule would come out of the session that begins in January and would apply to Kentucky hemp manufacturers, shippers and retailers holding state licenses.

Why it matters: If adopted, the changes would cut a Kentucky hemp-beverage maker's annual licensing cost by roughly two-thirds and reopen the on-premises channel — worth tracking for any distributor or retail partner building a Kentucky hemp program, though all of it is contingent on Congress acting before the Dec. 11 federal THC limits.

Federal

Rep. Barr details proposed federal hemp framework: age limits, testing, per-serving potency caps, packaging limits and dual taxes

FEDERAL • Louisville Public Media • Oct 15

Rep. Andy Barr (R-KY) said his bipartisan hemp bill — which has not yet advanced in Congress — would set a 21+ age limit, require third-party testing and manufacturing standards, cap THC potency per serving, bar bright-colored packaging and labels featuring characters, animals, vehicles, candy or fruit, and create dual taxes based on retail price and beverage potency. Barr said hemp growers need predictability and that the industry "want[s] to be viewed as a responsible and mature industry that will have a future"; Cornbread Hemp co-founder Jim Higdon said "the legality of our industry is under threat" in Washington. Some observers expect either new hemp rules or a temporary reprieve to ride on the next budget bill, since the current continuing resolution expires the same day the federal THC limits take effect, Dec. 11.

Why it matters: A potency-based federal beverage tax and the packaging restrictions would set the compliance cost structure for every hemp-beverage SKU — the Barr framework and the separate Sheehy-Klobuchar draft are the two live alternatives to the Dec. 11 limits.

Around the States

Kentucky medical cannabis dispensaries now open in all 11 licensing regions; House Majority Whip asks attorney general to intervene

KENTUCKY • Marijuana Moment • Oct 16

Gov. Andy Beshear (D) announced that 26 dispensaries are now open across all 11 of the state's licensing regions, with 46 licensed businesses in the supply chain, more than 28,000 approved patients and over 500 registered practitioners — growth that followed a June executive order adding 15 qualifying conditions after the legislature did not expand the list. House Majority Whip Jason Nemes (R) asked Attorney General Russell Coleman to ensure state agencies do not cooperate with that executive-order expansion, which he called "unlawful"; Beshear defended the program and the expansion.

Why it matters: The legal dispute over the qualifying-conditions expansion creates licensing uncertainty for Kentucky's newly statewide cannabis retail channel — partners with accounts in that channel should watch the attorney general's response.

South Dakota medical marijuana operators and oversight committee clash over speaker selection as 2027 recommendations take shape

SOUTH DAKOTA • Marijuana Moment / South Dakota Searchlight • Oct 15

At the legislature's Medical Marijuana Oversight Committee meeting Tuesday, industry representative Ned Horsted of 605 Cannabis said the panel invited mostly out-of-state critics of cannabis to testify; committee chair Rep. Josephine Garcia (R-Watertown) said the committee has "done a very good job" meeting its statutory duties, including recommending patient-access legislation. A House bill to eliminate the committee failed in the Senate this session, and the committee's recommendations will feed the state's 2027 medical cannabis legislation.

On the Radar

Oct. 21 — DEA's reply (capped at 10 pages) is due on rescheduling opponents' motion for new testimony and discovery; the proceeding remains stayed until Chief ALJ Derek Julius rules.

Nov. 3 — Massachusetts votes on Question 8, the measure that would repeal licensed commercial cannabis sales; 62 state legislators announced opposition last week.

Nov. 12 / Dec. 11 — The federal ban on synthetic (non-naturally occurring) cannabinoids takes effect Nov. 12; the 0.3% total-THC standard and 0.4 mg per-container cap for finished hemp products follow Dec. 11. Illinois enforces its 0.4 mg cap from Nov. 12.
