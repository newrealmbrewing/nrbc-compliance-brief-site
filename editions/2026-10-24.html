Top Story

DEA registration rules force staffing choices at marijuana retailers seeking Schedule III protections

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Oct 23

DEA correspondence shared by Nicole Huff, CEO of Mississippi's Wildflower Medical Dispensary, shows the agency told her business it must dismiss two employees with past controlled-substance felony convictions — or withdraw its registration application, give up expedited processing to seek an employment waiver, or go before an administrative law judge. DEA registration rules bar employing anyone with access to controlled substances who has such a conviction; the agency allows waivers but has published no timeline for deciding them. Sen. Cory Booker (D-NJ) called the policy “really problematic” and said records for nonviolent conduct that is now legal should be expunged. The registration form launched in April after the attorney general moved state-licensed medical marijuana products to Schedule III.

Why it matters: Retail partners weighing federal Schedule III registration should audit staff conviction records before filing — requesting an employment waiver forfeits expedited processing, and with no published waiver timeline, the choice can mean staffing changes or a slower path to federal protections.

Around the States

Oklahoma's top medical marijuana regulator steps down; compliance chief named interim director

OKLAHOMA • MJBizDaily • Oct 23

Gov. Kevin Stitt announced Thursday that Adria Berry, executive director of the Oklahoma Medical Marijuana Authority since 2021, is stepping down; she stays in the administration through the end of Stitt's term in January 2027. James Yingling, a former sheriff and highway patrolman who serves as OMMA's deputy chief of compliance and enforcement, becomes interim director, with a permanent chief to be named by the next governor. Stitt credited Berry with a “clear track record of results”; Berry said the administration's support “made it possible to root out bad actors.”

Why it matters: Oklahoma licensees face a transition period with an enforcement-career interim chief at the helm and a permanent appointment deferred to the next administration — partners there should expect continuity on inspections and enforcement priorities into 2027.

Minnesota opens $10.9 million CanRenew grant round; businesses among eligible applicants

MINNESOTA • Office of Cannabis Management • Oct 22

The Office of Cannabis Management opened applications October 22 for $10.9 million in CanRenew Community Restoration Grants, funding economic development, public health, violence prevention, youth development and civil legal aid projects in communities whose residents can qualify as social equity applicants. Private businesses, nonprofits, Tribes, educational institutions and local governments may apply — individually or in partnership — for an estimated 15 to 50 awards of $50,000 to $2,000,000. Applications close at 4:30 p.m. CT on December 15.

On the Radar

Oct. 28: TTB's online filing systems (Permits Online, COLAs Online, Formulas Online) will be unavailable Wednesday from 8:00 to 11:59 p.m. ET for maintenance — plan label and permit filings around the outage.

Oct. 29 / Nov. 5: Final written briefs in the DEA marijuana rescheduling proceeding are due October 29, with replies November 5; the proceeding stays paused until briefing closes.

Dec. 11: The federal 0.4 mg total-THC-per-container limit for hemp products takes effect nationwide (Illinois's own cap starts Nov. 12, with manufacturer license applications due Nov. 9). In Congress, H.R. 10079, the Beverage Regulatory Parity Act — which would keep hemp beverages up to 5 mg THC per serving legal under TTB oversight with three-tier distribution — remains before the House Energy & Commerce and Ways & Means committees.
