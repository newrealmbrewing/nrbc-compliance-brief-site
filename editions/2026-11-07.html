Top Story

Illinois businesses face Wednesday deadline to pull hemp THC drinks as breweries sell down stock

ILLINOIS • Chicago Sun-Times • Nov 7

The state law Gov. JB Pritzker signed in June caps hemp THC at 0.4 mg per container starting Wednesday, November 12, moving stronger products exclusively into licensed cannabis dispensaries — Illinois kept its own date rather than wait for the federal December 11 cutoff. Chicago brewers are winding down infused lines: Marz Community Brewing founder Ed Marszewski said "We're going to have to lay people off," Hopewell Brewing is selling its Choom inventory at 50% off, and Revolution Brewing halted its Reverb THC line over the summer. The Illinois Healthy Alternatives Association and several hemp businesses have filed an emergency motion for a temporary restraining order, arguing the law is unconstitutional.

Why it matters: Illinois retailers, venues and distributors must clear non-compliant hemp THC beverages by Wednesday — a compressed sell-through or return window — and unless the court intervenes, that inventory can only move through the dispensary channel afterward.

Federal

December 11 hemp deadline holds as officials and industry stake out positions after the midterms

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 6

Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker, Lt. Gov. Juliana Stratton and Sen. Tammy Duckworth published a congressional agenda calling for the federal recriminalization of hemp THC products to take effect December 11 without further delay. On the other side, Texas Hemp Business Council executive director Mark Bordas told KSAT he believes the administration supports extending the deadline. No bill moving the date has advanced.

Why it matters: Whether December 11 holds determines if hemp THC beverages above 0.4 mg THC per container can stay in national distribution past that date — plan inventory on the current deadline, not on a possible extension.

Around the States

Missouri AG promises more retailer lawsuits as judge lets state hemp ban stand

MISSOURI • Spectrum News / Missouri Independent • Nov 6

Attorney General Catherine Hanaway announced consumer-protection lawsuits against four retailers accused of selling marijuana products as legal hemp, saying "You're going to see more lawsuits." A federal judge dismissed the Missouri Hemp Trade Association's challenge to House Bill 2641, which takes effect November 12 and caps finished hemp products at 0.4 mg THC per container; beverages are exempt until December 11. Why it matters: Missouri retail partners have until Wednesday to pull non-compliant hemp products, but compliant hemp beverages keep their shelf access through December 11.

Texas hemp industry sees path back to shelves after statewide election results

TEXAS • KSAT • Nov 7

Texas Hemp Business Council executive director Mark Bordas put the odds that the full range of hemp THC products returns to Texas shelves at "around seven out of 10" after Lt. Gov.-elect Vikki Goodwin — whose stated priorities include legalizing cannabis, hemp and THC and reversing the state's regulatory crackdown — defeated Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick, who had said "I'm not going to legalize marijuana in Texas." Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa has criticized the state's current THC regulations.

Advocates expect Wisconsin legalization bill early in 2027 session under new trifecta

WISCONSIN • Cannabis Business Times • Nov 4

Marijuana Policy Project's Kevin Caldwell said "I think Wisconsin is going to move in 2027" after Democrats won the governorship — Gov.-elect David Crowley supports adult-use legalization — and both legislative chambers, the party's first trifecta since 2010. A pending Democratic bill from Sen. LaTonya Johnson would legalize medical and adult-use cannabis; related proposals would also set rules for intoxicating hemp products. Why it matters: how Wisconsin writes those rules will decide which retail channels — liquor, grocery or dispensary — carry hemp and THC beverages there.

On the Radar

Nov 12: State hemp THC caps take effect in both Missouri (HB 2641) and Illinois; Missouri hemp business owners plan a November 11 protest at the state Capitol.

Dec 11: The federal hemp recriminalization takes effect, capping hemp products at 0.4 mg THC per container nationwide; Missouri's hemp-beverage exemption ends the same day.

Jan 15, 2027: Deadline to reformulate any alcohol beverage containing FD&C Red No. 3 — TTB's standing guidance reminds producers the dye will no longer be permitted in foods, including alcohol beverages.
