Good morning — here's the regulatory picture for alcohol and hemp/THC beverages, readable in about three minutes. Every headline links to the original source.

TOP STORY

White House asks Congress to keep some hemp products legal as 14 GOP senators urge letting the Dec. 11 ban stand

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 24

An administration document sent to lawmakers says language is needed in the year-end funding bill to update the statutory definition of final hemp-derived cannabinoid products so that nonintoxicating full-spectrum CBD products remain accessible, with guardrails for children; it does not specify which products would be exempt. The same day, 14 Republican senators led by Sen. Ted Budd (R-NC) — including Sens. McConnell, Cotton, Lankford and Grassley — wrote to President Trump urging him to let the scheduled Dec. 11 recriminalization of hemp-derived THC products take effect, writing that the administration pledged not to seek further extensions and asking him to hold to that commitment. Sens. Budd and Ricketts have said they will not support a funding bill that reverses the ban, while Sen. Cotton said another delay would threaten the entire bill.

Why it matters: The December 11 effective date now hinges on the year-end funding negotiation: a narrow nonintoxicating-CBD carve-out would still end sales of hemp THC beverages, so distributors and retailers carrying those products should plan inventory and returns around the ban taking effect as scheduled.

FEDERAL

Total Wine & More asks Congress to choose regulation over the hemp THC ban

FEDERAL • KTTC • Nov 24

Total Wine & More is urging customers to contact Congress ahead of the December 11 federal hemp THC ban, which bars products with more than 0.4 milligrams of THC per container. Eric Brewer, the chain's senior vice president of government relations, said “Congress needs to choose regulation, not prohibition,” calling for rules requiring U.S.-grown hemp and a three-tier production, distribution and retail system similar to alcohol's, and describing THC products as roughly a $30 billion industry and the retailer's fastest-growing category.

Why it matters: A major national retail account is publicly campaigning on the ban; wholesale partners carrying THC beverages face the same December 11 cutoff and may see similar customer-facing campaigns across retail.

North Dakota, Utah and West Virginia back marijuana businesses in DEA rescheduling challenge

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 24

The three states, led by Utah Attorney General Derek Brown (R), filed an amicus brief supporting medical marijuana businesses challenging DEA registration restrictions issued under rescheduling, arguing a November 29 sourcing deadline and a retroactive “Timely Applicant” classification disrupt state markets and violate the Administrative Procedure Act. DEA's brief calls the challenges without merit and says the businesses have no reliance interests in violating the Controlled Substances Act.

DEA adjusts 2026 drug production quotas; marijuana levels unchanged for now

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 24

A DEA Federal Register notice raises 2026 production quotas for several research compounds, leaves Schedule I marijuana quotas unchanged, and says quotas for Schedule III medical marijuana under rescheduling are still being assessed and will be set in a separate notice. The notice also sets a first quota for hemp-derived HHC and consolidates cannabimimetic drug codes; comments are open for 30 days after publication.

AROUND THE STATES

North Carolina advisory council drafts plan for state-controlled marijuana retail modeled on its ABC system

NORTH CAROLINA • Marijuana Moment • Nov 24

A draft recommendation from the Advisory Council on Cannabis, appointed by Gov. Josh Stein (D), would create an adult-use market in which a state agency modeled on North Carolina's ABC liquor system licenses private contractors to run retail stores while the state controls procurement, distribution, pricing and tax collection; cultivation and processing would remain private. The council is expected to vote in the coming weeks, with a final report due December 31.

Why it matters: A state-run retail and distribution tier would shape who can hold licenses and how products reach shelves in a top-10 beverage market; wholesalers and retail partners in the Carolinas should watch the December 31 report.

Utah moves medical cannabis rules from health department to agriculture department

UTAH • Utah DHHS • Nov 24

Utah DHHS proposed repealing six medical cannabis rules (the R383 series) after House Bill 389 transferred regulation of the program to the Utah Department of Agriculture and Food, which plans to readopt the rules in early 2027. Public comments are due December 15; licensees and registrants will answer to a new regulator but the program's substantive rules are expected to carry over.

ON THE RADAR

Dec. 11 — Federal hemp THC ban takes effect: products over 0.4 mg total THC per container become illegal nationwide unless Congress acts in the year-end funding bill, now the main vehicle for any change.

Nov. 29 — DEA sourcing deadline for medical marijuana businesses under the rescheduling rules; three consolidated challenges and a state amicus brief are pending before the D.C. Circuit.

Hemp Guardrails Act — The Sheehy–Klobuchar bill to stop the ban, raise the hemp threshold to 1% total THC by dry weight, set a 21+ age limit and require FDA rules within 180 days remains pending in the Senate.
