Top Story

Pennsylvania Senator Unveils Statewide Hemp-THC Regulation Framework

PENNSYLVANIA • Marijuana Moment • Sep 28

Sen. Dawn Keefer (R) announced the Pennsylvania Hemp and Cannabinoid Act, a bill to bring consumable hemp-derived THC products — a market that currently operates in the state with no statewide age, testing, or labeling requirements — under a uniform framework: sales restricted to adults 21 and over with ID verification, mandatory registration with the Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture, independent product testing, truthful-labeling standards, and a ban on candy-style packaging and youth-targeted marketing. Keefer said the products today "are sold next to candy and snacks." Existing operators with documented pre-enactment operations would be grandfathered; the bill creates no new regulatory body and leaves medical cannabis rules unchanged. Registration fee amounts were not specified in the announcement and will be set in the bill text.

Why it matters: A registration-and-testing regime would create new compliance obligations — and eventual registration costs — for every Pennsylvania distributor and retailer carrying hemp-THC beverages; the fee schedule by tier is the number to watch when the bill is filed.

Federal Judge Dismisses Patco Brands' Final Counterclaims Against BuzzBallz

FEDERAL COURTS • Brewbound • Sep 28

A federal judge dismissed the last of Patco Brands' counterclaims against BuzzBallz, ending a three-year legal fight between the two rival makers of orb-shaped ready-to-drink cocktails.

Why it matters: Trade-dress outcomes in the fast-growing RTD segment shape how closely competing packaging can track a category leader's — a live question for suppliers and the retailers who set the shelf.

Around the States

Sixth Circuit Narrows Injunction Against Ohio's Hemp-THC Restrictions

OHIO • Forbes • Sep 26

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit narrowed a district-court injunction that had barred Ohio from enforcing Senate Bill 56's amended hemp definition against companies selling hemp-derived products, ruling the order was overly broad while leaving parts of it in place. SB 56 treats products above 0.4 milligrams of total THC per container as adult-use cannabis that may be sold only through licensed dispensaries; ten out-of-state hemp companies had challenged the law as a violation of the dormant Commerce Clause.

Why it matters: Most hemp-THC beverages exceed 0.4 mg per container, so Ohio retail accounts outside the dispensary channel again face enforcement exposure on those SKUs while the case proceeds.

California Governor Signs Bills Expanding Cannabis Testing and Track-and-Trace

CALIFORNIA • Marijuana Moment • Sep 28

Gov. Gavin Newsom signed AB 1965, which requires testing or retesting of all cannabis products, authorizes the Department of Cannabis Control to pull products for independent laboratory checks, and requires retailers to provide certificates of analysis on request; and AB 2250, which expands the state's track-and-trace program across cultivation, manufacturing, distribution, and delivery — and prohibits businesses that sell cigarettes or tobacco from selling cannabis.

Why it matters: The tobacco-retailer prohibition draws a hard line for California convenience and liquor accounts weighing licensed cannabis product sets alongside existing tobacco business.

On the Radar

Dec. 11, 2026 — The federal redefinition of hemp takes effect, removing legal status for most intoxicating hemp-derived THC products (those above 0.4 mg total THC and cannabinoids synthesized from hemp-derived CBD); Congress moved the date from Nov. 12 to Dec. 11 in the September stopgap funding law, per an Akerman client alert. Synthetic cannabinoids remain on the original November timeline.

Late October — The Virginia Cannabis Control Authority's preliminary-feedback window on its draft adult-use retail regulations approaches, with final rules due in December, license applications opening Feb. 1, 2027, and retail sales launching July 1, 2027.

Nov. 3 — Massachusetts voters decide the ballot question that would repeal retail cannabis sales; a poll covered in yesterday's edition showed voters opposing repeal 71–24.
