Top Story

Trump signs funding bill delaying federal hemp THC ban to Dec. 11

FEDERAL • White House • Sep 2

President Trump on Wednesday signed H.R. 6500, the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, which funds the federal government through December 11 and moves the effective date of the federal ban on hemp-derived THC products from November 12 to that same December 11 date. The House passed the bill 370–48 on Tuesday following earlier Senate approval; Brewbound described the change as a one-month reprieve for the hemp beverage sector. Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-MN) said the delay “is good news for Minnesota’s farmers, brewers, small businesses, and customers,” per FOX 21; opponents of the delay, including Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), have pressed instead for permanent hemp legislation.

Why it matters: Hemp THC beverages remain federally lawful to produce, ship and sell through December 11 — inventory, production and distribution planning now runs to that date, with state law still controlling market by market.

Federal

With the ban delayed, bipartisan lawmakers file hemp regulation bills

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Sep 2

Rep. Morgan Griffith (R-VA) introduced the HEMP Act to set federal guardrails, saying producers and consumers deserve “a system that upholds oversight, responsibility and safety”; Reps. Beth Van Duyne (R-TX) and Greg Landsman (D-OH) filed a bill to keep hemp THC drinks legal while taxing and regulating them like alcohol, and Reps. James Comer (R-KY) and Andy Barr (R-KY) are advancing proposals covering testing, packaging, age-21 limits and taxation. The same reporting notes the delay does not extend to synthetic cannabinoids, which remain set for recriminalization on November 12.

Why it matters: The Van Duyne–Landsman approach would place hemp THC beverages under an alcohol-style tax-and-regulate framework — the licensing and distribution regime brewers, distributors and retailers already operate in.

Around the States

New Mexico judge rules this morning on tracking-system challenge as NMS2S goes live

NEW MEXICO • KUNM • Sep 1

The state’s new NMS2S track-and-trace system went live for retailers Tuesday after a six-day sales blackout during the cutover, and Second Judicial District Judge Joshua Allison said he would rule Thursday morning on operators’ emergency motion after observing the system’s first days. The Cannabis Association of New Mexico’s Matt Kennicott said the blackout meant “no cash flow for us,” while state lawyers argued the plaintiffs had not demonstrated genuine harm.

Why it matters: Partners selling THC products in New Mexico face reporting and inventory obligations in NMS2S as of this week; today’s ruling determines whether any part of the cutover is unwound.

California enacts limits on cannabis packaging “attractive to children”

CALIFORNIA • Marijuana Moment • Aug 31

Gov. Gavin Newsom (D) signed AB 2249, by Assemblymember Jacqui Irwin (D), prohibiting cannabis packaging and ads that use cartoons, candy or cereal imagery, bubble lettering, or imitations of children’s products, effective January 1, 2028; realistic fruit depictions on edibles remain permitted. The California Cannabis Industry Association opposed the measure, saying it “would impose significant costs on licensed businesses.”

Why it matters: THC products sold in California will need a packaging-compliance review before 2028 — a long lead time, but label redesigns and printed-inventory runs should be planned against it.

On the Radar

Nov. 12: The recriminalization of synthetic cannabinoids keeps its original date — only hemp-derived THC products moved to December 11, per Marijuana Moment.

Sept. 17 / Oct. 8: Possible Travis County temporary-injunction hearing dates on Texas’s “Total THC” rules (0.3% limit counting THCA, higher license fees), with a TRO ruling pending, per FOX 7 Austin.

DEA rescheduling: The Schedule III proceeding remains pending after summer hearings; DEA’s docket page tracks the regulatory actions, and registrants are completing the agency’s 26-question protections form circulated this week.
