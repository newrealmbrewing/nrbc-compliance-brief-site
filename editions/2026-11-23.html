Top Story

Appeals court consolidates industry challenges to DEA marijuana rescheduling rules ahead of Nov. 29 deadline

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 20

Four vertically integrated Pennsylvania medical cannabis companies — G.P.M. II, MLH Explorations, Tri-Mountain Pure and Story of PA CR — petitioned the D.C. Circuit to stay DEA guidance issued October 30, which gives federally registered cannabis businesses until November 29 to source product only from other operators that registered or applied for registration on time. The petitioners said the guidance is “procedurally deficient, contrary to law and arbitrary and capricious,” arguing it skipped notice-and-comment rulemaking and would bar their manufacturing divisions from supplying even their own dispensaries. The court consolidated the case with a similar petition filed a day earlier by operators in Utah, Arizona and Oklahoma; DEA has not publicly responded to the suits.

Why it matters: Any supplier or retail partner touching federally registered cannabis supply chains faces the November 29 counterparty cutoff unless the court stays it — confirm the registration status of trading partners now, and watch for a ruling that could move the date on short notice.

Around the States

Georgia proposes overhaul of medical cannabis patient registry rules; comments due Dec. 9

GEORGIA • Ga. Dept. of Public Health • Nov 20

The Department of Public Health filed proposed revisions to Chapter 511-5-11 that would rename “low THC oil” terminology to “medical cannabis,” require physicians to certify a patient's continuing diagnosis annually, allow electronic registry cards subject to appropriations, and prohibit ingestion in any public place (smoking remains prohibited; vaporization is barred for ages 21 and under). The notice proposes no change to card fees — the registration card fee stays as posted on the department's website, with replacements at the same fee. Written comments are due December 9, a virtual public hearing is set for December 8 at 2:00 PM, and the commissioner is expected to consider adoption on or about December 18.

Why it matters: Georgia retail partners serving registry cardholders should plan for annual recertification checks and electronic-card verification at the counter once the rules are adopted.

South Carolina senator says health department must reschedule marijuana under state trigger law

SOUTH CAROLINA • Marijuana Moment • Nov 20

Sen. Tom Davis (R) wrote to the Department of Public Health's acting director arguing the agency is violating two state statutes by not mirroring the federal move of marijuana to Schedule III — state law gives officials 30 days to reschedule a substance after a federal change, a deadline Davis says passed months ago. He asked the department to issue a rescheduling order, implement the state's 1980 therapeutic research program, and answer within 30 days, and he requested a formal opinion from the attorney general's office. The department has not publicly responded.

Why it matters: If the agency acts, South Carolina would reschedule marijuana at the state level without new legislation — a template other trigger-law states may follow, and a market-access question Southeast distributors should track.

On the Radar

Dec. 11 — The federal limit of 0.4 mg total THC per container takes effect, removing most hemp THC beverages from lawful sale absent congressional action; the first phase covering lab-made cannabinoids such as THC-O took effect Nov. 12. The bipartisan Hemp Guardrails Act in the Senate and a House delay bill remain pending with no floor action scheduled.

Nov. 29 — DEA's counterparty registration deadline for federally registered cannabis businesses — now the subject of the consolidated D.C. Circuit litigation above; a stay request is pending.

Dec. 8–9 — Georgia's virtual public hearing (Dec. 8, 2:00 PM) and written-comment deadline (Dec. 9) on the medical cannabis patient registry revisions above.
