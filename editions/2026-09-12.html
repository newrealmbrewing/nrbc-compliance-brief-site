Top Story

Nebraska Supreme Court rejects constitutional challenge to voter-approved medical cannabis laws

NEBRASKA • Marijuana Moment / Nebraska Examiner • Sep 11

The Nebraska Supreme Court unanimously rejected a challenge to the state's voter-approved medical cannabis laws, ruling that former state Sen. John Kuehn lacked standing to bring the case. Kuehn had argued the laws conflict with the U.S. Constitution's supremacy clause given marijuana's federal status; Chief Justice Jeffrey Funke wrote that Kuehn "did not have either taxpayer standing or standing for a matter of great public concern." The dismissal was without prejudice, and the state's Medical Cannabis Commission continues writing rules and licensing cultivators, with retail access expected in the coming months.

Why it matters: Another legal cloud over a new state cannabis market has cleared on procedural grounds — Nebraska's program build-out and licensing timeline stay on track, which is the operative planning fact for suppliers and retail partners watching that market open.

Federal

DEA registration conditions under Schedule III come into focus, including employment screening

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Sep 11

DEA correspondence to a Mississippi dispensary seeking federal registration under the Schedule III rescheduling shows expedited processing conditioned on not employing individuals with drug-related felony convictions, per existing DEA registrant rules; the agency outlined three options — seek an employment waiver (losing expedited status), withdraw the application, or proceed to an administrative hearing. The dispensary's CEO, Nicole Huff, said she will request waivers for the two affected employees and pursue a hearing. The report notes DEA inspection and interview practices so far vary across regional offices.

Why it matters: This is one of the first concrete looks at the compliance conditions attached to federal registrations under rescheduling — businesses on the cannabis side of the aisle should expect DEA-style employment screening, recordkeeping and site requirements as the price of federal protections.

Around the States

Georgia: medical cannabis patients may now vaporize — but not smoke — under expanded law

GEORGIA • Marijuana Moment • Sep 9

Under Georgia's Putting Patients First Act, registered patients 21 and over may now use dry-herb and oil vaporization, and the law drops the old "low THC oil" labeling and prior potency caps while adding qualifying conditions; smoking raw flower remains illegal — bill sponsor Sen. Matt Brass said "the only thing that's allowed in inhalables is vaporizing." Nearly 50,000 patients were enrolled as of September 2.

Why it matters: New Realm's home-state medical THC market is maturing quickly, a factor in how Georgia regulators and lawmakers approach adjacent categories — including hemp beverages — in the next session.

California: regulators announce cannabis recall over pesticide violations and unsanitary production

CALIFORNIA • Marijuana Moment • Sep 11

California cannabis regulators announced a product recall tied to unsanitary production conditions and pesticide violations, part of a steady drumbeat of state product-safety enforcement; New Jersey regulators separately imposed a $10,000 fine in licensing and disciplinary actions the same week.

On the Radar

September excise filings — TTB's September 4 newsletter reminds semimonthly excise taxpayers that September has three return periods: the accelerated September 16–26 period is paid within September, and the September 27–30 period is due October 14.

December 11, 2026 — Effective date for the new federal hemp restrictions, after the September 2 law delayed the original November 12 deadline; hemp-derived THC beverage makers have roughly ninety days of runway to adjust portfolios and inventory.

November — Massachusetts voters decide Question 8, a statewide measure on rolling back adult-use marijuana sales, and U.S. House action on hemp regulation is expected the same month; DEA and FDA implementation steps on the Schedule III order continue in the interim.
