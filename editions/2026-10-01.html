Top Story

DEA judge pauses marijuana rescheduling hearing after opponents move to enter GAO report

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Sep 30

DEA Chief Administrative Law Judge Derek Julius stayed the marijuana rescheduling hearing after three parties opposed to reform — the National Drug and Alcohol Association, DUID Victim Voices and Dr. Kenneth Finn — moved to enter a recent Government Accountability Office report into the record. The GAO report found the DEA "does not have any policies or procedures regarding how its staff are to conduct evaluations or schedule substances." DEA and the other parties must file responses by October 13 on whether to reopen the hearing record; the judge will then decide before issuing his recommendation on moving marijuana to Schedule III.

Why it matters: the pause pushes the rescheduling decision — and the federal tax and banking changes that would follow it — further out, extending planning uncertainty for any beverage business weighing cannabis-adjacent investments.

Federal

NIQ and MRI-Simmons data put intoxicating hemp beverages at a $300 million business

NATIONAL • Brewbound • Sep 30

Research shared in a webinar by NIQ, MRI-Simmons and the Dry Atlas database values the intoxicating hemp beverage market at $300 million, with relaxation and stress relief the top reasons consumers cite for drinking the category. The figures land as the industry faces the delayed federal hemp ban, now set to take effect December 11 absent further congressional action. Why it matters: retailers and distributors carrying the category now have a national benchmark for its size — and a hard federal date against which to plan inventory and supplier commitments.

Around the States

Keg 1 River City to acquire RNDC's Kentucky business as the distributor's wind-down continues

KENTUCKY • Brewbound • Sep 30

Brewbound reports that Keg 1 River City will take over Republic National Distributing Company's Kentucky business. Under the letter of intent announced in August, the deal adds roughly 2 million annual cases and is expected to close in the fourth quarter, pending bankruptcy-court approval and regulatory clearances, as RNDC conducts a Chapter 11 wind-down of its remaining operations. Why it matters: Kentucky retail accounts and suppliers routed through RNDC will see their wine and spirits distribution consolidate under Keg 1 River City — contract assignments and service terms are worth confirming before the Q4 close.

Georgia's medical cannabis commission takes up final adoption of mail-delivery rules

GEORGIA • Cannabis Business Times / Marijuana Moment • Sep 30

The Georgia Medical Cannabis Commission met Wednesday with an adoption vote scheduled on rules letting the state's six production licensees ship medical cannabis to Georgia's roughly 45,000 registered patients by USPS or approved couriers — with tracking, identity verification and signature on delivery required. Commission Executive Director Andrew Turnage called delivery "one of the more exciting changes" for patients facing geographic barriers. Why it matters: in New Realm's home state, mail delivery would open a statewide channel for licensed medical cannabis products without adding retail storefronts.

Campaign launches ads against Massachusetts ballot measure that would end licensed cannabis sales

MASSACHUSETTS • Marijuana Moment • Sep 30

The "No on 8: Stop the Repeal" campaign released its first ads against Question 8, the November measure that would repeal laws allowing regulated commercial cannabis sales and home cultivation while keeping possession and the medical program legal. In one ad, former public health commissioner Alan Balsam said the measure "creates real risk, forcing people to buy products that aren't regulated." Recent polls show opposition ranging from 55% to 71%; supporters of Question 8 argue commercial sales have gone further than voters intended.

Pennsylvania Senate majority leader says cannabis legalization unlikely this session

PENNSYLVANIA • Marijuana Moment • Sep 30

Senate Majority Leader Joe Pittman (R) said he does not expect adult-use legalization to pass this session, noting that "recreational marijuana, for worse or better, is a scheduled narcotic at the federal level" and questioning the House-passed state-store model. House Speaker Joanna McClinton (D) said last week the state could legalize next year if Democrats win Senate control in November, and Gov. Josh Shapiro (D) has again asked lawmakers to send him a legalization bill.

On the Radar

October 13 — DEA and the other rescheduling-hearing parties must file responses on whether the record reopens for the GAO report; the administrative law judge's recommendation on Schedule III waits on that ruling.

December 11, 2026 — the delayed federal hemp ban's effective date. H.R. 6500, signed September 2, pushed most restrictions back from November 12; the ban on synthetic cannabinoids still takes effect November 12, 2026. Absent further congressional action, hemp-derived THC beverages lose federal lawful status on December 11.

Late October — the Virginia Cannabis Control Authority's preliminary-feedback window on its draft adult-use retail regulations approaches, with final rules due in December and license applications opening February 1, 2027 (covered in full in our September 23 special edition).
