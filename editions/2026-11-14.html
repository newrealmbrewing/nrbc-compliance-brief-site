TOP STORY

New Jersey extends legal hemp beverage sales until the federal ban takes effect

NEW JERSEY • Office of the Governor • Nov 12

Gov. Mikie Sherrill (D) signed S-4635/A-5604 on Wednesday — one day before the state's own cutoff — aligning New Jersey's restrictions on hemp products and intoxicating hemp beverages with federal law. Under the prior rule, hemp drinks above 0.4 milligrams of THC per container would have moved to dispensary-only sales on November 13; the new law moves that cutoff to December 11, or later if federal implementation is delayed again (NJ 101.5). The bill passed the Senate 37-0 and the Assembly 75-2.

Why it matters: New Jersey liquor and grocery accounts keep roughly another month of lawful hemp-beverage sales, and the state deadline now tracks the federal one — December 11 is the single sell-through date to plan against in New Jersey.

FEDERAL

Senate panel's spending bill renews state medical cannabis protections, omits House anti-rescheduling language

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 13

The Senate Appropriations Committee released its FY2027 Commerce-Justice-Science bill Thursday, renewing the rider in place since 2014 that bars the Justice Department from using funds to interfere with state medical marijuana programs — and leaving out the House version's provision that would have blocked funds for rescheduling marijuana. The Senate bill also carries a separate rider protecting state hemp research programs; committee votes have not been scheduled.

Why it matters: the Senate text leaves the marijuana rescheduling process on its current track — the proceeding whose outcome shapes banking, tax and investment terms across the cannabis-adjacent beverage sector.

Total Wine & More CEO, citing new poll, asks Congress to regulate THC beverages through the three-tier system

FEDERAL • Total Wine & More release • Nov 11

Total Wine & More CEO Ryan Ross said nearly 300,000 customer messages have been sent to Congress supporting access to low-dose hemp-derived THC beverages, and asked lawmakers to regulate them through the existing three-tier alcohol system — with age-gated sales, product testing, potency limits and U.S.-grown hemp — before the December 11 federal deadline. A company-commissioned ChangeMakers Intelligence poll of 2,001 registered voters (Oct. 30–Nov. 2) found 85% of respondents oppose a ban on the beverages.

Why it matters: the country's largest alcohol retailer is advocating a framework that would route hemp beverages through existing alcohol wholesalers and licensed retailers — the channel this readership already operates.

AROUND THE STATES

Incoming Texas officials put THC regulation on the 2027 session agenda

TEXAS • Marijuana Moment • Nov 12

Lt. Gov.-elect Vikki Goodwin (D) said regulating THC is among her priorities for the session that opens in January 2027, saying she is “not interested in a full ban on THC” but has not settled on an approach; Agriculture Commissioner-elect Clayton Tucker (D) called hemp “our saving grace” for farmers. Outgoing Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick (R) had pushed a hemp THC ban that Gov. Greg Abbott (R) vetoed last year, and the restrictive agency rules adopted afterward remain under court challenge.

Why it matters: Texas hemp-beverage rules are likely to be rewritten starting in January — a market-access question worth watching for any partner with Texas distribution.

ON THE RADAR

Nov. 19 — U.S. District Court hearing in Chicago on the Illinois hemp industry's restraining-order and preliminary-injunction motions; the state's enforcement pause for the ~250 lawsuit businesses expires the same day (CBS Chicago).

Dec. 11 — second phase of the federal hemp definition change: products above 0.4 mg of total THC per container lose federal hemp status nationwide. Pending alternatives include the Griffith–Veasey HEMP Act, the Van Duyne–Landsman beverage bill, and an expected Sheehy–Klobuchar proposal that supporters hope to attach to must-pass legislation before the deadline (Marijuana Moment).

FDA cannabinoid lists — the FDA remains more than nine months past the statute's February 10 deadline to publish the list of naturally occurring cannabinoids and the definition of “container” that determine exactly what the two ban phases cover; the American Trade Association for Cannabis and Hemp says the guidance will “settle the edges of this category” (Marijuana Moment).
