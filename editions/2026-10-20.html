Top Story

Supreme Court agrees to review the 1868 federal ban on home distilling

FEDERAL • Courthouse News • Oct 19

The Supreme Court on Monday granted review in a challenge brought by the Hobby Distillers Association and Texas hobbyist Scott McNutt to the Reconstruction-era law that makes distilling spirits at home a federal crime. The Fifth Circuit held the ban exceeds Congress's enumerated powers and blocked its enforcement against the plaintiffs, while the Sixth Circuit rejected a parallel Ohio challenge on standing grounds eleven days earlier; the Justice Department asked the Court to take the Texas case. Challengers argue Congress used its taxing power to criminalize purely local conduct; the government defends the ban as part of the excise-tax system for distilled spirits.

Why it matters: The ruling will test how far Congress's taxing power reaches in regulating alcohol production — the same constitutional footing beneath the federal excise and permit system that producers, distributors and retailers operate under every day.

NORML asks Sixth Circuit to reconsider ruling that cannabis contracts are unenforceable

FEDERAL COURTS • Marijuana Moment • Oct 19

NORML filed an amicus brief urging the full Sixth Circuit to revisit last month's panel ruling, in a Michigan dispute involving Curaleaf subsidiaries, that federal courts cannot enforce cannabis supply contracts because of federal prohibition. The group argued the decision threatens commercial accountability in state-regulated markets; Curaleaf CEO Boris Jordan said the company was pleased with the panel's ruling.

Why it matters: If the panel ruling stands, supply and purchase agreements with cannabis-licensed counterparties in the Sixth Circuit (MI, OH, KY, TN) may not be enforceable in federal court — a contracting risk for any partner doing business alongside that channel.

THC drink maker launches campaign to send six-packs to members of Congress

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Oct 19

Beverage company Herb Light said customers who buy a 12-pack can add a six-pack to be delivered to their members of Congress with a letter supporting pending legislation that would keep hemp THC drinks federally legal under a regulatory framework. The company said its drinks contain 1 mg THC and that it would produce a 0.4 mg version if the federal limits take effect as written.

Around the States

Illinois proposes 45 new infuser licenses with extra lottery entry for hemp businesses

ILLINOIS • Illinois News Joint • Oct 19

The Illinois Department of Agriculture published proposed rules (Illinois Register, Oct. 16) to issue at least 45 new cannabis infuser licenses — the manufacturer tier that covers infused edibles and beverages sold through licensed dispensaries — beginning January 1, 2027. License and fee structure: applicants pay a nonrefundable $2,500 pre-application fee (per application); winners selected by lottery if applications exceed 45; applicants with qualifying hemp-business experience under the Industrial Hemp Act receive one additional lottery entry; and Social Equity Experience Lottery licensees pay no license renewal fee for their first two annual renewal cycles. Applicants must meet at least two social-equity criteria. Comments are open through November 30.

Why it matters: This is a new entry path into Illinois infused-beverage manufacturing priced at $2,500 up front with two years of waived renewal fees — a materially lower cost of entry for hemp beverage operators weighing a pivot into the licensed dispensary channel before the federal THC limits land.

New Jersey advances criminal penalties for unlicensed cannabis and vapor sales

NEW JERSEY • Cannabis Business Times • Oct 19

The Senate Judiciary Committee unanimously advanced S4287, which would make commercial sale of unlicensed cannabis a third-degree crime with fines of $10,000–$50,000 for individuals and $100,000–$250,000 for business entities, let police seize noncompliant products as contraband without a warrant, and add unlicensed cannabis and vapor-product activity to the state RICO act. A companion bill, S4635, would align New Jersey's hemp restrictions with the federal timeline and keep hemp sales by ABC and Cannabis Regulatory Commission licensees legal until the federal changes take effect.

Why it matters: Licensed New Jersey retailers would gain enforcement leverage against unlicensed competitors, while any store selling vapor or hemp products off-channel would face six-figure entity fines and racketeering exposure.

Colorado auditor finds gaps in marijuana product-safety testing and inspections

COLORADO • Axios Denver • Oct 19

The Colorado Office of the State Auditor reported that most marijuana products sold from 2023 through 2025 were not consistently tested for contaminants or potency, and that potentially harmful products remained on shelves for months in 2025 before the Marijuana Enforcement Division issued an advisory. Auditors made 14 recommendations; the division agreed to partially implement them by December 2027.

On the Radar

Tomorrow, Oct. 21: The DEA's deadline to respond to rescheduling opponents' request to reopen the hearing record over the September GAO report; the proceeding remains stayed before the agency's chief administrative law judge.

Also Oct. 21: Oregon's OLCC holds a public hearing on rules giving hemp-item registry applicants more chances to fix application deficiencies; comments close Oct. 30 at noon.

Nov. 12 / Dec. 11: The federal hemp timeline holds — products with cannabinoids not naturally produced by the plant are barred Nov. 12, and the broader redefinition, including the 0.4 mg total-THC-per-container limit, takes effect Dec. 11.
