Top Story

Hemp beverage trade group "optimistic" a federal solution emerges before the ban, but says "miniature prohibition" is possible

FEDERAL • Brewbound • Oct 16

Hemp Beverage Alliance president and CEO Christopher Lackner said he is "optimistic" about the prospect of a federal legislative solution for hemp beverages emerging before the federal hemp restrictions take hold, while cautioning that a "miniature prohibition" remains possible — the category is now less than two months from a ban on nearly all products currently on the market unless Congress acts. Among the proposals already filed is the bipartisan Beverage Regulatory Parity Act from Reps. Beth Van Duyne (R-TX) and Greg Landsman (D-OH), which would move hemp THC beverages into alcohol-style three-tier distribution with a per-milligram THC excise tax, a 21+ age minimum, and a ban on synthetically derived cannabinoids.

Why it matters: Absent congressional action, hemp products above 0.4 mg total THC per container lose federal protection on Dec. 11 (with the synthetic-cannabinoid change on Nov. 12) — distribution and retail partners carrying THC beverages should plan Q4 orders, shelf resets and sell-through around those dates.

Federal

Trade court certifies nationwide importer class for IEEPA tariff refunds

NATIONAL • Steptoe client alert • Oct 13

The Court of International Trade on Oct. 8 certified a nationwide class in Freestyle World, Inc. v. United States covering importers that paid duties under the challenged IEEPA tariff orders between Feb. 1, 2025 and Feb. 19, 2026, excluding those that already filed their own refund suits. The firm notes that no steps are currently required to be included in the class, that the government may appeal, and that refund mechanics for non-litigants remain unresolved.

Why it matters: Businesses that paid IEEPA-era duties on imported beverages, ingredients, packaging or equipment may now have a refund path without filing suit — keep customs entry records organized and coordinate with your broker.

Around the States

Coors Distributing and Constellation square off in court over terminated distribution rights

COLORADO • Brewbound • Oct 16

In the litigation over Constellation Brands' termination of Denver's Coors Distributing Co., the wholesaler argued the termination presents a "substantial threat" to its business, while Constellation countered that the dispute is a "classic money damages case." The outcome will help define what protection distributors have when a supplier pulls its brands mid-agreement.

Oklahoma packaging rules, including THC-serving labels on cannabis drinks, take effect Nov. 1

OKLAHOMA • KOSU • Oct 16

Under House Bill 4454, Oklahoma Medical Marijuana Authority rules effective Nov. 1 require edibles and marijuana beverages to display the number of THC servings per package and THC content per serving, and bar packaging resembling people, cartoons, animals or candy. Transfers of noncompliant product to dispensaries are prohibited from Nov. 1, though dispensaries may sell through existing inventory.

Why it matters: Processors and brands selling into Oklahoma dispensaries must have compliant labels on anything transferred on or after Nov. 1 — the compliance point is the transfer date, not the sale date.

On the Radar

Tomorrow, Oct. 21 — DEA's response is due on anti-rescheduling parties' request to reopen the marijuana rescheduling record; the proceeding remains stayed before the agency's chief administrative law judge.

Nov. 12 / Dec. 11 — The federal hemp changes phase in: the reclassification of synthetic cannabinoids takes effect Nov. 12, and the broader 0.4 mg total-THC-per-container limit on Dec. 11; Illinois has said it will enforce its own hemp THC limits beginning Nov. 12 regardless of the federal delay.

Nov. 3 — Massachusetts voters decide Question 8, which would end retail cannabis sales statewide; dozens of state lawmakers, including the House speaker and Senate president, have urged a "no" vote, and the result will determine whether the state's licensed retail market continues.
