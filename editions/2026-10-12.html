Top Story

Connecticut reaches $2 million settlement with wholesaler over unlicensed THC product distribution

CONNECTICUT • Conn. Office of the Attorney General • Oct 9

Attorney General William Tong announced a stipulated judgment with RZ Smoke, Inc., a wholesale distributor accused of supplying high-potency THC products to smoke shops, gas stations and convenience stores statewide without a license from the Department of Consumer Protection. The judgment totals $2 million — $375,000 payable to the state and $1.6 million suspended, conditioned on compliance — and bars the company from manufacturing, packaging, selling, distributing or storing intoxicating THC products in Connecticut unless licensed.

Why it matters: Enforcement is reaching the wholesale tier, not just store shelves — retailers carrying hemp-THC products should be able to document that every supplier in the chain is licensed in-state, and distributors handling these SKUs face direct financial exposure for unlicensed sales.

Federal

Consumer group asks Congress to let the Dec. 11 hemp-THC deadline take effect

FEDERAL • National Consumers League • Oct 8

The National Consumers League said Congress "should do nothing to alter the closure of the hemp loophole," asking lawmakers to leave the December 11, 2026 effective date in place until what it called a robust regulatory regime for hemp-derived products is established, citing poison-control and emergency-department reports. The ask is the opposite of the Marijuana Policy Project's campaign, covered in Sunday's edition, which urges Congress to halt the ban and adopt a regulatory framework instead; both sides are pressing lawmakers with the deadline two months away.

Why it matters: With organized advocacy now running in both directions, planning for hemp-beverage inventory, contracts and promotions should not assume another extension of the Dec. 11 date.

Around the States

Arkansas Supreme Court: Kirkland Signature beverages are one brand, must use a single wholesaler

ARKANSAS • Vinetur • Oct 9

In Arkansas Wine and Spirits Wholesale, LLC v. Department of Finance and Administration (No. CV-23-544, decided Oct. 8), the court held that Kirkland Signature products constitute a single brand under Arkansas distribution law even when different manufacturers produce them, so one wholesaler must handle the brand in the state; at least one justice dissented.

Why it matters: In franchise states, private-label programs that spread a shared brand name across multiple producers may be locked to a single wholesaler — a factor for suppliers and distributors structuring retailer-brand deals.

Colorado regulators expand health advisory on an unapproved synthetic flavoring agent

COLORADO • Marijuana Moment • Oct 12

The Marijuana Enforcement Division expanded a health and safety advisory covering regulated cannabis products made with a synthetic, unapproved flavoring agent, broadening last week's guidance on inactive ingredients. Producers using flavoring systems across beverage and edible lines should confirm ingredient approvals with the state before shipping affected batches.

On the Radar

Dec. 11, 2026 — The federal THC limits on hemp-derived products take effect, barring further congressional action. State agencies (Rhode Island among them, per Sunday's edition) are drafting conforming guidance; watch for transition rules on existing inventory.

Nov. 3, 2026 — Massachusetts votes on Question 8, the ballot measure that would end retail cannabis sales in the state; several state senators have said publicly they oppose it.

CBMA 2027 assignments — TTB's latest newsletter notes the opening of 2027 assignments under the Craft Beverage Modernization Act; importers claiming reduced excise rates should get supplier assignments on file early.
