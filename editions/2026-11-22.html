Good morning — here's the regulatory picture for alcohol and hemp/THC beverages, readable in about three minutes. Every headline links to the original source. A quieter weekend news cycle, so today's edition runs a few items lighter than usual.

Top Story

Texas governor-elect says she will revoke Abbott's hemp THC executive order

TEXAS • Marijuana Moment • Nov 20

Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa (D) said she plans to revoke the executive order Gov. Greg Abbott (R) issued restricting hemp-derived THC products — the order behind the state's age-21, labeling and retail-enforcement push — and told The Texas Tribune that Texans "deserve" an election on marijuana legalization. Texas has no citizen-initiative process, so a ballot measure would require a two-thirds joint resolution in both chambers, where Republicans retain the Senate majority. Lt. Gov.-elect Vikki Goodwin (D) said legally regulating THC products will be a top priority in the 2027 session.

Why it matters: Texas is one of the largest hemp-beverage markets in the country, and a January revocation would ease state-level compliance pressure on retailers there — but the federal ban still takes effect Dec. 11 regardless of state policy, so inventory planning should assume the federal deadline controls.

Federal

Senate appropriators would keep DOT cannabis testing in place under rescheduling

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 19

The Senate Appropriations Committee's fiscal 2027 bills direct the Department of Transportation to "maintain current drug testing requirements for cannabis" for truck drivers, pilots and other safety-sensitive workers even if marijuana is rescheduled, and continue the rider barring Washington, D.C. from legalizing recreational sales. The committee declined to adopt House language that would block rescheduling and proposed extending the state medical marijuana protections rider.

Why it matters: DOT drug-and-alcohol testing obligations for CDL drivers — including distributor delivery fleets — would stay exactly as they are even if rescheduling goes through.

Second lawsuit filed over DEA's rescheduling trade restrictions; cases consolidated

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Nov 20

Four Pennsylvania medical cannabis companies petitioned the D.C. Circuit to stay DEA counterparty restrictions scheduled to take effect Nov. 29, arguing the rules were issued without notice and comment; the court has consolidated the case with a similar petition filed last week.

TTB joins international talks on digital labeling and no/low-alcohol beverages

FEDERAL • Wine Industry Advisor • Nov 20

TTB's International Affairs Division took part in the World Wine Trade Group meeting in Stellenbosch, South Africa (Nov. 1–4), contributing to discussions on digital labeling principles and guidance for calculating calorie content in no- and low-alcohol beverages; the group adopted voluntary sustainability guidelines. No change to U.S. labeling rules was announced.

Around the States

Wisconsin's incoming leaders float legalization — and an emergency hemp fix — before Dec. 11

WISCONSIN • Marijuana Moment / Wisconsin Examiner • Nov 21

Incoming Democratic legislative leaders and the governor-elect said legalizing marijuana could help preserve Wisconsin's hemp businesses as the Dec. 11 federal hemp ban approaches, and lawmakers said an emergency hemp fix is under discussion; the state's 2019 hemp law does not shield products from the federal ban.

Why it matters: Wisconsin retailers have already been clearing THC drink inventory; any state response would come after the federal deadline, so near-term purchasing decisions there still hinge on Dec. 11.

On the Radar

Dec. 11 — The broader federal ban on most hemp-derived THC products takes effect unless Congress acts; the first phase took effect Nov. 12, and the Sheehy–Klobuchar Hemp Guardrails Act remains pending with a short congressional calendar.

December — TTB is changing the mailing address for paper excise tax returns and checks; updated forms with the new address are expected this month. If you mail excise payments, watch for the new address or move to Pay.gov.

Dec. 7 — Comments are due on TTB's information-collection renewals (No. 99), which include the "Usual and Customary Business Records Maintained by Brewers" collection.
