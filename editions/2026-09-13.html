Good morning — here's the regulatory picture for alcohol and hemp/THC beverages, readable in about three minutes. Every headline links to the original source. A quiet weekend on the regulatory wire, so today's edition runs lighter than usual.

Top Story

Constellation Brands portfolio to exit Coors Distributing Company in Colorado

COLORADO • Brewbound • Sep 11

Constellation Brands notified Coors Distributing Company, Molson Coors' company-owned distributor in Denver, that Constellation's portfolio of Mexican import brands will leave the house within 30 days. Neither company has publicly stated a reason for the termination, and the brands will need new representation in the Denver market.

Why it matters: A supplier move of this size typically brings inventory transitions, shelf resets and new ordering contacts; Colorado retail and wholesale partners should confirm supply continuity during the 30-day window.

Federal

Dispensary owner says DEA registration conditions require removing employees with felony drug convictions

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Sep 11

Under the administration's move of state-licensed medical marijuana to Schedule III, businesses applying for federal protections must detail security, inventory, suppliers and personnel with access to controlled substances. Nicole Huff, CEO of a Mississippi dispensary, said DEA told her two employees with drug-related felony convictions must be removed or receive waivers — and that seeking waivers forfeits expedited processing. She said she plans to request the waivers and may bring the question before an administrative law judge.

Why it matters: Personnel vetting is emerging as part of the practical cost of Schedule III registration, alongside security and inventory obligations, for any business weighing federal protections.

Around the States

Illinois licenses 37 adult-use dispensaries to serve medical cannabis patients

ILLINOIS • Illinois Dept. of Financial & Professional Regulation • Sep 10

IDFPR issued medical cannabis dispensary licenses to 37 existing adult-use dispensaries, effective immediately, allowing them to serve registered patients at the medical tax rate at the same location — a change the agency called the biggest expansion of the state's medical market in a decade. Secretary Mario Treto Jr. said the move means "better options and increased convenience for medical patients, while still maintaining regulatory safeguards."

On the Radar

Dec. 11 — New effective date for the federal hemp THC ban, after President Trump signed the delay Sept. 3 (Marijuana Moment). The ban would limit hemp products to 0.4 mg total THC per container; synthetic cannabinoids not naturally producible from the cannabis plant are still recriminalized Nov. 12. Lawmakers are using the window to weigh regulatory alternatives.

Nov. 3 — Massachusetts votes on Question 8, which would repeal the state's laws legalizing, regulating and taxing adult-use marijuana (The Boston Globe). The Coalition for a Healthy Massachusetts backs the measure; the industry-funded Stop the Repeal campaign opposes it.

September excise filings — TTB's latest newsletter reminds semimonthly excise taxpayers that September has three return periods (TTB); brewers filing on that schedule should confirm this month's due dates.
