Top Story

Convenience-store industry asks Congress to regulate hemp THC products, not ban them

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Sep 7

With the federal hemp THC ban now set to take effect December 11, the convenience-store industry is pressing Congress to replace prohibition with a regulatory framework that would keep the products on shelves for adults. Jon Taets, director of government relations at NACS, the convenience-store trade association, said the channel has "well-established age verification procedures" suited to legal adult sales; several pending bills would regulate hemp products along alcohol-style lines, with age limits, packaging rules, and testing standards.

Why it matters: Convenience is one of the largest retail channels for hemp beverages — whether Congress lands on regulation or lets the December 11 ban take effect will determine shelf access for THC drinks across that channel and its distributor partners.

Federal

CRS report: circuit split on cannabis licensing rules raises odds of Supreme Court review

FEDERAL • Marijuana Moment • Sep 7

A new Congressional Research Service analysis says conflicting appeals-court rulings on state residency requirements for cannabis business licenses "likely increases the odds" the Supreme Court eventually takes the issue. The First and Second Circuits have held such requirements violate the dormant Commerce Clause; the Ninth Circuit declined to apply it, reasoning there is no constitutional right to engage in federally illegal interstate commerce.

Why it matters: A Supreme Court ruling on residency rules would shape whether multistate operators — including beverage companies eyeing cannabis licenses — can enter states that currently reserve licenses for residents.

Around the States

Texas judge rejects restraining order; hemp enforcement continues as industry suit proceeds

TEXAS • Marijuana Moment • Sep 8

A Texas judge rejected hemp businesses' request for a temporary restraining order against the state's hemp product restrictions, allowing enforcement to continue while their lawsuit proceeds. Texas moved in late July to recategorize delta-8 and similar hemp-derived THC products as controlled substances, with delta-9 products under 0.3% dry weight remaining legal; industry challenges are continuing in both state and federal court.

Why it matters: Texas is one of the largest hemp beverage markets in the country — while the restrictions stay in force, distributors and retailers there face continued enforcement exposure on most THC products.

On the Radar

Dec. 11, 2026 — The federal hemp ban takes effect, capping total THC at 0.4 mg per container and removing most consumable hemp products from the legal market. A Bradley client alert notes FDA has yet to publish its covered-cannabinoid list or define "container," and that pending bills range from full repeal to a two-year delay to an FDA regulatory framework and the Van Duyne–Landsman hemp beverage bill.

DEA rescheduling — The agency's review of moving marijuana to Schedule III remains pending, with registrant protections and licensing questions unresolved; a decision would also influence how courts handle the licensing disputes flagged in today's CRS report.

Sep. 17 — Minnesota's Office of Cannabis Management holds a public listening session in Moorhead with regulatory updates, market information, and Q&A — a venue for hemp beverage makers and retailers in that market to raise rule concerns directly with regulators.
