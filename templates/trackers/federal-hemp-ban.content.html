Where Things Stand

The FY2026 appropriations law enacted in November 2025 rewrote the federal definition of hemp. When the change takes effect, products containing more than 0.4 milligrams of total THC per container — a threshold most hemp-derived THC beverages, gummies and vapes exceed — lose their status as federally legal hemp, and cannabinoids synthesized from hemp-derived CBD are excluded from the definition entirely. This replaces the 2018 Farm Bill's 0.3% delta-9 THC dry-weight standard as the operative federal line for consumable hemp products, per Akerman's client alert.

Congress has moved the effective date once: the stopgap funding law H.R. 6500, signed September 3, 2026, pushed the main restrictions from November 12 to December 11, 2026 (Forbes). Akerman notes the delay applies to non-synthetic cannabinoids; provisions on synthetic cannabinoids remain on the original November 2026 timeline. Whether Congress delays again, amends the definition, or lets it take effect is the open question the industry is watching.

Timeline

  • November 2025 — Enacted

    Appropriations law redefines federal hemp

    The FY2026 appropriations package signed in November 2025 amends the hemp definition to exclude consumable products above 0.4 mg total THC per container and cannabinoids synthesized from hemp-derived CBD, with a one-year runway before the change takes effect.

  • September 3, 2026 — Signed

    H.R. 6500 delays the main restrictions one month

    The Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027 moves the effective date for non-synthetic cannabinoid restrictions from November 12 to December 11, 2026 (Forbes; Akerman).

  • November 12, 2026 — Upcoming

    Synthetic-cannabinoid provisions take effect

    Per Akerman's reading of H.R. 6500, the delay covers non-synthetic cannabinoids only; synthetic cannabinoids remain subject to the original November 2026 timeline.

  • December 11, 2026 — Upcoming

    The new hemp definition takes effect

    Absent further congressional action, products above 0.4 mg total THC per container stop qualifying as federally legal hemp on this date. State law continues to govern separately — several states restrict these products earlier or more tightly than the federal timeline.

Dates reflect enacted law as reported by the linked sources as of the last-updated date above; pending bills and future continuing resolutions can move them. This page tracks the federal definition — state rules (caps, bans, licensing) apply on top of it and are covered in the state-by-state index.

What It Means for Beverage Businesses

  • Producers and brands: hemp-THC beverages above 0.4 mg total THC per container lose their federal hemp status on December 11, 2026 — reformulation, state-licensed cannabis channels, or exit become the options in scope-affected markets.
  • Distributors: inventory and contract exposure turns on the effective date and on each state's own rules; the federal change does not preempt states that already regulate or ban these products.
  • Retailers: shelf decisions after December 11 depend on how enforcement agencies treat existing stock and on state law — several states have their own caps (for example, Virginia's 2 mg-per-package limit, in effect since August 2026) that bind regardless of the federal date.
